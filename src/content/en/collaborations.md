## Overview

Scientific progress rarely happens in isolation. Throughout the 2026 iGEM season, we worked with fellow iGEM teams, educational organizations, community partners, and institutions to extend our project's reach beyond the laboratory.

Our collaborations took many forms. Some partners shared their scientific knowledge through mentorship and educational workshops. Others helped us develop competitions, organize public events, or bring synthetic biology to students who might otherwise have little exposure to the field. Each collaboration let us contribute something of our own while learning from others' experience, expertise, and perspectives.

One of our most important goals was to make synthetic biology more accessible across the Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region. We wanted to connect teams working in different countries, strengthen regional scientific communication, and create opportunities for students to engage with synthetic biology in Arabic. At the same time, we looked beyond our region, partnering with organizations in Peru and Kazakhstan and working with local communities across Saudi Arabia.

These collaborations became an essential part of our work in education, public engagement, and human practices.

## MENA iGEM Collaborations

The Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region is home to a growing community of young scientists and innovators. Yet, opportunities for iGEM teams across the region to connect, share resources, and communicate their research to broader audiences remain limited.

We saw an opportunity to change that.

What began as individual collaborations with other iGEM teams gradually developed into Tawasol (تواصل), a regional initiative designed to bring teams together around science communication, education, and public engagement. The name _Tawasol_ means connection or communication in Arabic. It reflects our belief that scientific collaboration should extend beyond exchanging technical information. It should also create relationships, build shared resources, and make scientific knowledge accessible to the communities we serve.

Through our partnerships with NYU Abu Dhabi, Al-Nahrain University, King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM), and Armed Forces College of Medicine (AFCM) Egypt, we worked toward a more connected regional iGEM community.

## Global Educational Collaborations

<div class="card-grid stack">

<div>

#### Kallpa Greenz, Peru

We formed a partnership with Kallpa Greenz, a Peruvian company that builds greenhouse domes using modern technology across various climates in Peru. KALLPA has over 70 volunteers working in different areas (construction, marketing, business opportunities, logistics, education, among others). SHIHAB designed and developed a Spanish language training program on GMOs for KALLPA’s young volunteers, with the goal of transferring this knowledge to farmers in rural areas of the country, ultimately reaching approximately 180 farmers trained across three different communities.

</div>

<div>

#### Jeddah Global Shapers

We connected with Jeddah Global Shapers to explore opportunities for community-based science education and youth engagement. Our discussions focused on combining our team’s scientific expertise with the organization’s community outreach network to make biotechnology more accessible to young people in Jeddah.

</div>

<div>

#### Oboor Sister Program

We collaborated with Renad Almalki, a counseling psychology master’s researcher at Umm Al-Qura University, to recruit students for the Oboor Mentorship Sister Program as a companion to our iGEM Mentorship Program.

While our mentorship initiative introduced students to scientific research and academic opportunities, Oboor focused on preparing Saudi students for the personal and educational transition from high school to university. Together, the programs supported students’ scientific interests and broader academic development.

</div>

## Growing Through Collaboration

Our collaborations showed us that scientific impact extends beyond the laboratory. Fellow iGEM teams brought new ideas and expertise, educational partners helped us reach diverse audiences, and institutional collaborators contributed the resources and perspectives needed to turn our initiatives into reality.

From developing educational resources with Kallpa Greenz to building a five-team regional network through Tawasol, each partnership strengthened our ability to communicate science, engage communities, and learn from others.

<div class="callout accent">

Together, these experiences reinforced a central lesson of our iGEM journey: meaningful scientific progress is built not only through what we discover, but through what we share.
