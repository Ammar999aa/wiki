## S. cerevisiae Recombineering — Media & Stock Solutions

The following reagents and solutions are required for all four LiAc/SS-DNA/PEG protocols.

- **YPD medium and plates** - Used to maintain yeast strains under non-selective conditions.
  - 20 g/L Bacteriological peptone
  - 10 g/L Yeast extract
  - 20 g/L Bacteriological agar (for plates)
  - Add 960 mL of deionized water, autoclave for 15 minutes, and add 40 mL of a 50% sterile (0.2 mm filtered) solution of glucose.

- Lithium Acetate (1.0 M). Dissolve 5.1 g of lithium acetate dihydrate (Sigma Chemical Co. Ltd., St. Louis, MO) in 50 ml of water, sterilize by autoclaving for 15 min, and store at room temperature.

- Polyethylene Glycol 3350 (50%, w/v). Dissolve 50 g of PEG 3350 (Sigma) in 30 ml of distilled, deionized water in a 150-ml beaker on a stirring hot plate. When the solution has cooled to room temperature, make the volume up to 100-ml in a 100-ml measuring cylinder and mix thoroughly by inversion. Pour the solution into a suitable glass bottle and autoclave for 15 min. Store, securely capped, at room temperature. Evaporation of water from the solution will increase the concentration of PEG and severely reduce the yield of transformants.

- Single-Stranded Carrier DNA (2.0 mg/ml). Dissolve 200 mg of salmon sperm DNA (Sigma) in 100 ml of TE buffer (10 mM Tris-HCl, 1 mM Na2 EDTA, pH 8.0) by stirring at 4° for 1-2 hr. Store 1.0-ml samples at -20°. Denature the carrier DNA in a boiling water bath for 5 min and chill in ice/water before use. Boiled samples, stored at -20°, can be reused or reboiled three times without loss of activity.

- **Yeast Growth Media.** Yeast strains are grown up on plates of YPAD agar (YPD supplemented with 100 mg adenine hemisulfate per liter). The yeast cells to be transformed are usually regrown in liquid 2× YPAD medium (2% Bacto-yeast extract, 4% Bacto-peptone, 4% glucose and adenine hemisulfate, 100 mg/liter).

- 2x YPAD = double-strength YPAD broth

- **SC selection medium** is adjusted to pH 5.6 with 1.0 N NaOH and autoclaved.

- The routine **nonselective medium** is a complex medium called YEPD (for yeast extract peptone dextrose). Per liter it is composed of 10 g yeast extract powder and 20 g peptone. After autoclaving, sterile glucose is added to a final concentration of 2% (w/v). For petri plates, agar is added before sterilization to a final concentration of 2% (w/v).

- The **defined medium** is called SD (for synthetic dextrose). Per liter it is composed of 6.7 g of yeast nitrogen base (without amino acids). After autoclaving, glucose is added to a final concentration of 2% (w/v). For plates, the solution is made up at 2× concentration and after sterilization it is mixed with an equal volume of sterile molten 4% (w/v) agar after the solutions are below 65°.

- Add sterile glucose to a final concentration of 2% (w/v). For most purposes, the SD medium is supplemented with a complete set of the growth supplements commonly required by different auxotrophs. Growth on this rich defined medium is much faster than on minimal medium. A powder mixture is prepared containing 2.0 g each of all of the amino acids except leucine, of which 4.0 g is added. To this add 0.5 g adenine, 2.0 g uracil, 2.0 g inositol, and 0.2 g p-aminobenzoic acid.

- If selection of a gene for a particular growth supplement is desired, that supplement is left out of the mixture. A couple of clean marbles is added to the powder and it is mixed end over end for at least 15 min. Use 2 g of the powder mixture per liter of SD. Autoclave with the yeast nitrogen base. Other carbon sources can be used instead of glucose. For a nonfermentable carbon source glycerol and ethanol can be used at 2% (v/v). For a carbon source that does not cause catabolite repression, use the nonfermentable compounds or raffinose at 2% (w/v).

## S. cerevisiae Strain Maintenance

- S. cerevisiae strains can be stored at 4 °C for 2–3 months on YPD or SC plates sealed with Parafilm.
- For long term storage, scrape a large inoculum from a freshly grown plate and resuspend in 1 mL of sterile 15-25% glycerol.
- Store at -70 °C.
- To revive frozen yeast stocks, scrape frozen stock with a sterile toothpick or bacteriological needle, and streak on the appropriate plate (YPD or SC). Avoid thawing the stock. If the stock has thawed, vortex well before refreezing.

## LiAc/ssDNA/PEG Transformation — Gietz & Woods Protocol

**Protocol for the LiAc/single-stranded carrier DNA/PEG method developed by Gietz and Woods**¹¹

1. Inoculate the yeast strain into 5 ml of liquid medium and incubate overnight at 30°C.

   **Liquid medium:** (2x YPAD or synthetic complete [SC] selection medium)

2. Place a bottle of (2x YPAD) and 250 ml culture flask in the incubator as well.
3. Take 10 µl of the cells added to 1.0 ml of water in a spectrophotometer cuvette.
4. Measuring the OD₆₀₀. - 0.3

   **Note:** For many yeast strains, a suspension containing 1 × 10⁶ cells/ml will give an OD₆₀₀ of 0.1.

5. Transfer 50 ml of the pre-warmed 2x YPAD to the pre-warmed culture flask and add 2.5 × 10⁸ cells to give a density of 5 × 10⁶ cells/ml.
6. Incubate the flask on a rotary or reciprocating shaker at 30°C and 200 rpm.

   **Note:** It is important to allow the cells to complete at least 2 divisions. Transformation efficiency remains constant for 3 to 4 cell divisions).

7. After 4h measure the OD₆₀₀.
8. When the cell titer is at least 2 × 10⁷ cells/ml harvest the cells by centrifugation,
9. Wash the cells in 25 ml of sterile water, and wash again in 1 ml of sterile water.
10. Add water to a final volume of 1.0 ml and vigorous vortex-mixing to resuspend the cells. Pipette 100 µl samples (~10⁸ cells) into 1.5-ml microcentrifuge tubes, one for each transformation, centrifuge at top speed for 30 s, and discard the supernatant.
11. Add 360 µl of transformation mix, consisting of 240 µl PEG 3350 [50% (w/v)], 36 µl LiAc (1.0 M), 50 µl boiled single-stranded DNA (2.0 mg/ml), and 34 µl plasmid DNA plus water, to each transformation tube and resuspend the cells by vigorous vortex-mixing.
12. Incubate the tubes in a 42°C water bath for 40 min.

   **Note:** The optimum time can vary for different yeast strains].

13. Microcentrifuge at top speed for 30 s and remove the transformation mix with a micropipette. Pipette 1.0 ml of sterile water into each tube, stir the pellet with a micropipette tip, and vortex.
14. Plate appropriate dilutions of the cell suspension onto SC2 selection medium

## LiAc/ssDNA/PEG Transformation — Dr. Tehseen Protocol

**Protocol for the LiAc/single-stranded carrier DNA/PEG method (shared by Dr Tehseen)**

### Part 1: Competent cells preparation

1. Streak a YPD plate with your S. cerevisiae strain such that isolated, single colonies will grow. Incubate the plate at 28-30°C for 2 days.
2. Inoculate 10 ml of YPD with a single colony of your S. cerevisiae strain.
3. Grow overnight at 28-30°C in a shaking incubator (200-300 rpm).
4. Determine the OD600 of the overnight culture. It should be between 3.0 and 5.0.
5. Dilute cells from the overnight culture to an OD600 of 0.2 to 0.4 in a total volume of 10 ml of YPD.
6. Grow the cells at 28-30°C in a shaking incubator until the OD600 reaches 0.6 to 1.0. This will take approximately 3 to 6 hours.
7. Pellet the cells by centrifugation at 500 x g (1500 rpm) for 5 minutes at room temperature.
8. Discard the supernatant.
9. Resuspend in sterile dH2O
10. Pellet the cells by centrifugation at 500 x g (1500 rpm) for 5 minutes at room temperature.
11. Discard the supernatant.
12. Resuspend the cell pellet in 3ml of sterile 1.1XTE/LiAc solution
13. Split suspension in 2 1.5 ml tubes

Cells are now competent and can be used immediately for transformation or stored for future use

### Part 3: Freezing & Storing

1. To freeze cells, aliquot 50 to 200 µl of competent cells into labeled 1.5 ml sterile screw-cap microcentrifuge tubes. **Note:** 50 µl of cells are used for each transformation. Cells can be thawed and refrozen several times without significant loss in transformation efficiency.
2. Place tubes in a q or wrap in several layers of paper towels.
3. Place in a -80°C freezer.

**Note:** It is important that the cells freeze down slowly to avoid damage to the cell wall. Do not snap-freeze the cells in liquid nitrogen.

### Part 2: Transformation

1. For each transformation, thaw one tube of competent cells at room temperature and aliquot 50 µl into a sterile microcentrifuge tube, or use 50 µl of fresh competent cells.
2. Add 1 µg of vector DNA to the competent cells. **Note:** Using up to 5 µg of DNA may increase transformation efficiencies in some cases. The volume of DNA should not exceed 5 µl.
3. Add 600 µl of PEG/TE/LiAc solution to the DNA/cell mixture and mix by vortexing vigorously or by flicking the tube.
4. Incubate the transformation reactions for 30 min at 30°C, 200 rpm
5. Add DMSO to a final concentration of 10%, mix by gentle inversion
6. Heat shock for 15-30 min at 42 °C
7. Chill on ice for 1-2 min
8. Centrifuge for 5 sec at 14000 rpm
9. Add 1 ml of YPD (or YPAD if using the yeast strain L40) to each tube.
10. Incubate the cells in a 30°C shaker for 1 hour to allow expression of antibiotic resistance.
11. After the 1 hour recovery time, pellet the cells by centrifugation at 3000 x g for 5 minutes at room temperature.
12. Discard the supernatant.
13. Resuspend the cell pellet in 100 to 150 µl of appropriate medium or TE buffer
14. Using a sterile spreader, plate the entire transformation on appropriate plates containing antibiotics
15. Incubate the plates for 2 to 4 days at 30°C. Each transformation should yield approximately 100 colonies depending on the strain you are using.

1. Prepare 100 ml in a bottle after Autoclave and before use add 5.25 ml Glucose 40% (sterile) under sterile conditions.

1. From Glycerol stock inoculate 20-40 ul of the PichiaPink in 10 ml of YPD Media. Use the control. Incubate 30C for 48H. We did this step because our glycerol stock is old and it is more difficult to revive the PichiaPink.
2. Spread the culture on the plate YPD agar. Check after 48 hours and use only pink colonies.

### Step 2

1. Inoculate 1 colony in 125 ml sterile flask (10 ml of YPD media). You can make 2-3 colonies for quicker growing. Incubating 190 rpm 30C for 1-2 days. This is starter culture

### Step 3

1. Digestion to get a linear DNA with restriction enzyme that doesn't cut inside your gene (we have chosen bgl II) 50 ul reaction (10 ul water + 30ul plasmid + 5ul 10xbuffer + 5ul restriction enzyme) – 37C ON.

3. Purify the DNA.

1. Inoculate 1 ml of starter culture in 1 L flask with 100 ml YPD media. Grow the culture for 1-2 days.
2. Next day transfer cells in 2 - 50 ml sterile centrifuge tubes. Centrifuge 4C 1500 g for 5 minutes (2 min enough). Resuspend pellets in each tube with ice cold water (fill the tube and mix gently – washing the pellets) – then centrifuge the cells, remove water and wash the 2d time – centrifuge and remove the water.
3. Resuspend pellets in 10 ml of sterile ice cold sorbitol (1M). Centrifuge and remove supernatant
4. Resuspend the pellets in 300 ul of sorbitol to get a final volume of 500 ul.
5. Keep the cells on ice and use the same day for the transformation.

1. Gently mix the reaction by pipetting up and down and microfuge briefly.
2. Incubate at temperature and time ranges recommended for the specific restriction endonuclease.

## Electroporation Cuvette Cleaning Procedure

1. Immediately after electroporation
   - Aspirate the cell suspension.
   - Rinse the cuvette 5–10 times with large volumes (1–2 mL each) of nuclease-free or deionized water.
   - Flush water between the electrodes thoroughly.
2. Ethanol wash
   - Fill the cuvette with 70% ethanol.
   - Let sit for 5–10 minutes.
   - Discard and rinse once or twice with sterile water.
3. Final rinse
   - Rinse 3–5 times with nuclease-free water to remove any ethanol residue.
   - (Some labs perform a final rinse with 95–100% ethanol to facilitate drying.)
4. Dry completely
   - Shake out excess liquid.
   - Air-dry with the opening facing downward in a dust-free environment.
   - Alternatively, blow filtered compressed air through the gap.
   - Ensure no visible droplets remain between the electrodes before storage or reuse.
5. Storage
   - Store dry in a clean sealed container or sterile bag.
   - Avoid touching the electrode surfaces.

**Community reports describe successful reuse for approximately 5–10 transformations, sometimes more, although transformation efficiency can gradually decline and the chance of arcing increases.**

## B. subtilis Culturing and Maintenance

### Materials

- *Bacillus subtilis* glycerol stock or plate culture
- LB broth (Luria–Bertani medium)
- LB agar plates (optional, for streaking)
- Sterile culture tubes or flasks (e.g., 50 mL tube or 250 mL flask)
- Incubator shaker (set to 37 °C)
- Sterile inoculation loop or pipette tips
- Spectrophotometer (for OD₆₀₀ measurement)

### Media Preparation

1. Prepare LB broth:
   - Tryptone: 10 g/L
   - Yeast extract: 5 g/L
   - NaCl: 10 g/L
2. Adjust volume with distilled water.
3. Autoclave at 121 °C for 15–20 minutes.
4. Allow media to cool to room temperature before use.

### Procedure

**1. Inoculation**

From a glycerol stock: Scrape a small amount(loopful) of frozen stock and inoculate directly into 3–5 mL LB broth.

From a plate: Pick a single colony and inoculate into 3–5 mL LB broth.

**2. Overnight Culture**

1. Incubate the culture at 37 °C.
2. Shake at 180–220 rpm.
3. Grow for 12–16 hours.

**3. Subculture (Optional – for log phase growth)**

1. Dilute overnight culture 1:100 into fresh LB medium
   - Example: 100 µL culture into 10 mL LB
2. Incubate at 37 °C with shaking.
3. Monitor growth by measuring OD₆₀₀:
   - Log phase: OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.4–0.8
- *B. subtilis* grows rapidly; cultures may reach stationary phase within 6–8 hours.
- Ensure adequate aeration:
  - Use flasks filled to ≤20–30% of total volume.
- Antibiotics (if required) should be added after media cooling.

For sporulation studies, LB is not ideal; use specialized media instead.

## B. subtilis Anaerobic Culture Setup

### Materials

- *Bacillus subtilis* glycerol stock
- LB medium
- Mineral salt medium with glucose
- Sodium nitrate (NaNO3)
- Ammonium sulfate (NH4)2SO4
- Potassium phosphate buffer components:
  - KH2PO4
  - Na2HPO4·2H2O
- MgSO4·7H2O
- Trace element solution
- Sterile serum flasks (e.g. 120 mL)
- Butyl rubber septa and aluminum crimps
- Nitrogen gas source
- Syringes and needles
- Incubator shaker
- Spectrophotometer for OD600

### Media composition

Anaerobic mineral salt medium

For serum flask anaerobic culture, the paper used:

To prepare 1 L of anaerobic mineral salt medium for *Bacillus subtilis*, dissolve 5 g of glucose (or 2.5–10 g depending on experimental requirements), 4.08 g of KH₂PO₄, and 7.12 g of Na₂HPO₄·2H₂O in approximately 800 mL of distilled water to establish the buffering system. Add 8.5 g of sodium nitrate (NaNO₃) as the terminal electron acceptor and 0.066 g of ammonium sulfate ((NH₄)₂SO₄) as a nitrogen source. Supplement the medium with 0.20 g of MgSO₄·7H₂O, along with trace amounts of additional salts including 2.1 mg sodium citrate, 0.78 mg calcium chloride (CaCl₂), 1.1 mg FeSO₄·7H₂O, 0.17 mg MnSO₄·H₂O, and 0.97 mg Na₂MoO₄·2H₂O. Adjust the final volume to 1 L with distilled water and mix thoroughly. The medium can be sterilized by autoclaving, with heat-sensitive components such as iron salts or reducing agents added separately after cooling if required. For stricter anaerobic conditions, a small amount of resazurin may be included as a redox indicator and a suitable reducing agent can be added prior to use.

### Procedure

**1. First pre-culture**

1. Inoculate 20 mL LB medium in a 100 mL baffled flask with 100 µL glycerol stock of *B. subtilis*.
2. Incubate at 37 °C and 120 rpm overnight.

**2. Second pre-culture**

1. Dilute the LB overnight culture 1:100 into fresh mineral salt medium.
2. Incubate at 37 °C and 120 rpm for 36 h.
3. Keep flask filling volume at about 10–13% of total flask volume for proper mixing.

**3. Preparation of anaerobic serum flasks**

1. Clean and dry 120 mL serum flasks and butyl rubber stoppers.
2. Dispense the anaerobic mineral salt medium into the flasks.
3. Autoclave the buffer and nitrogen-source components in the flasks when possible; other sterile components may be added later through the septum using a syringe.

**4. Removal of oxygen**

1. Place the filled serum flasks in a hot water bath for about 20–30 min to reduce dissolved oxygen.
2. Flush the flask headspace thoroughly with nitrogen gas.
3. Immediately seal the flasks with butyl rubber septa and aluminum caps.
4. For stricter anaerobic conditions, add a reducing agent after flushing; 0.1 mL reducing agent per 50 mL medium and resazurin as a redox indicator.

**5. Inoculation**

1. Using a sterile syringe and needle, inoculate the anaerobic serum flask through the septum with the pre-culture.
2. Handle inoculation quickly to minimize oxygen entry.
3. After inoculation, keep the flasks sealed.

**6. Anaerobic incubation**

1. Incubate the inoculated serum flasks at 37 °C.
2. The general anaerobic-cultivation method recommends monitoring pressure after the flasks equilibrate to incubation temperature and releasing excess pressure if needed.
3. Growth can be slower than aerobic culture; in the *B. subtilis* anaerobic study, serum flask cultures were followed over many hours to days.

**7. Monitoring growth**

1. Withdraw samples aseptically through the septum using a syringe.
2. Measure OD600.
3. If needed, monitor nitrate, nitrite, ammonium, glucose, or acetate during the culture, since anaerobic nitrate respiration depends on nitrate reduction and nitrite can transiently accumulate.

## B. subtilis Recombineering

### Media Components

- SM1 medium (10× stock, diluted to 1× before use)
- SM2 medium (1× stock)
- Glucose stock solution (200 g/L)
- Calcium chloride (CaCl₂) stock solution (1 M)
- Luria-Bertani (LB) medium
- LB agar plates supplemented with appropriate antibiotics

### Methods

**Part I: Preparation of Overnight Culture**

1. Prepare SM1 medium supplemented with glucose to a final concentration of 5 g/L.
2. Inoculate 10 mL of SM1 medium with a single colony of *B. subtilis*.
3. Incubate overnight at 37°C with shaking at 250 rpm.
4. Ensure that incubation does not exceed 16 hours to maintain transformation efficiency.

**Part II: Preparation of Competent Cells**

1. Measure the optical density (OD₆₀₀) of the overnight culture.
2. Dilute the culture in fresh SM1 medium to a final OD₆₀₀ of 0.5 in a 250 mL flask.
3. Incubate at 37°C with shaking at 250 rpm for 3 hours.
4. Add 10 mL of SM2 medium to the culture.
5. Supplement the culture with:
   - 250 µL of 200 g/L glucose stock
   - 45 µL of 1 M CaCl₂ (added while gently swirling to ensure proper mixing)
6. Continue incubation at 37°C with shaking at 250 rpm for 2 hours.

**Note:** Cells are considered competent for approximately 1 hour following this step.

**Part III: Transformation**

1. Aliquot 500 µL of competent cells into sterile microcentrifuge tubes.
2. Add 250 ng to 1 µg of plasmid DNA to each aliquot.
3. Incubate the mixtures at 37°C for 30 minutes with shaking (~800 rpm).

**Part IV: Recovery**

1. Add 300 µL of LB medium to each transformation reaction.
2. Incubate at 37°C with shaking (~800 rpm) for at least 2 hours to allow expression of antibiotic resistance.

**Part V: Plating and Selection**

1. Plate up to 200 µL of each transformation mixture onto LB agar plates containing the appropriate antibiotic.
2. Incubate plates overnight at 37°C.

**Optional Steps**

- Increased transformation efficiency: Centrifuge transformation mixtures briefly (e.g., 6000 × g for 2 minutes) and resuspend in a smaller volume prior to plating.
- Storage of competent cells: Add an equal volume of 50% glycerol to competent cells and store at –80°C for future use.

## B. subtilis Sporulation

### Materials

DSM
LB broth
Shaking incubator
Spectrophotometer

**Note:** always use fresh DSM since the FeSO4 is rusting when it is in dilution

### Methods

**Part 0: DSM (Difco Sporulation Medium) preparation:**

- 8 g — Nutrient Broth
- 1 g — KCl
- 1 ml — MgSO4 (1 M)
- 1 ml — MnCl2 (10 mM)
- 1000 ml — Aqua bidest

Autoclave and add:
- 0.5 ml — CaCl2 (1 M)
- 1 ml — FeSO4 (1 M)

Additionally, add D-Glucose

**Part I: Overnight culture**

1. Inoculate B. subtilis sample in 4 ml LB broth
2. Incubate the sample overnight at 37°C, 200 rpm

**Part II: Exponential growth**

1. Measure the OD600 of overnight culture and dilute it in LB broth to an OD600 ~ 0.1-0.2/ml in 10 ml LB
2. Let the cells grow to an OD600 of 0.8/ml at 37°C, 200 rpm

**Part III: Sporulation**

1. Centrifuge 10 ml of the cells at 13,000 x g for 1 minute
2. Wash the pellet with 1x PBS
3. Resuspend the pellet in 5 ml DSM nutrient medium at 37°C, 200 rpm for 48-72 hours to achieve >95% sporulation

→ Alternative: Add 25 mL of overnight bacterial culture into a 2 L flask containing 250 mL of DSM and incubate at 37°C, 200 rpm for 48-72 hours to achieve >95% sporulation

4. Centrifuge the culture (10,000 g, 10 min) and wash the pellet 3 times with cold, sterile Milli-Q H2O to remove vegetative cell debris
5. Heat shock the suspension (80°C for 10-20 min) to kill any remaining vegetative cells.
6. Store purified spores in sterile ddH2O at 4°C

**Part IV: Validation**

1. The sporulation rate (the percentage of spores among total cells) can be determined by phase-contrast microscopy (100×, oil immersion)

## Agarose Gel Electrophoresis

**Preparing Agarose gel:**

1. Weigh 1g of agarose in a microwaveable beaker.
2. Add 100 mL 1XTAE (Tris-acetate-EDTA buffer) to the Agarose powder and mix.
3. Heat the mixture in the microwave until the powder is completely dissolved. (Avoid overheating.)
4. Cool down the Agarose mixture for 5 min until it reaches 50°C.
5. Add 2µl of red safe to 20ml of agarose solution, then pour the Agarose solution into the gel tray, and place the well comb. (The amount of agarose and red safe to use will change depending on your well size, this is for a small 12-well gel.)
6. Let it sit at room temperature for 20-30 mins until it solidifies.

**Running an Agarose Gel:**

1. Once solidified, put the agarose gel in the gel chamber.
2. Fill the gel chamber with 1XTAE (Tris-acetate-EDTA buffer diluted in water). Make sure the gel wells are covered.
3. Slowly load 5 µl of the molecular weight ladder into the first lane of the gel.
5. Carefully add 10 µl of your samples and controls into the wells of the gel
6. Run the gel at 100V until the dye has reached the middle of the gel.
7. Turn off the power and disconnect the electrode and slowly remove the gel from the gel box.
8. See the DNA fragment (bands) with any UV light machine.

## SDS-PAGE

**Preparing SDS Page gel (5 gels):**

1. For resolving buffer mix 3x gel buffer 30% Acrylamide (29.65 ml), 10% APS (300 µl), and THEMED (30 µl). (total volume 30 ml).
2. Set your gel vertically on a rack, then add the resolving buffer up to the 3rd horizontal line. (tap the cassette against the bench to get rid of an air bubble)
3. Carefully add isopropanol, 70% ethanol, or water up to the top and let it polymerize for 45 min to 1 h.
4. After the resolving gel is polymerized, carefully discard the ethanol (isopropanol or water) on the top.
5. for stacking buffer mix 3x gel buffer 30% Acrylamide (7.9 ml), 10% APS (80 µl), and THEMED (16 µl). (total volume 8 ml).
6. Add 1.5 ml of the stacking buffer on top of the resolving buffer.
7. Insert the comb into the gel cassette, let the gel polymerize for 30 min to 1 h.
8. After the gel is polymerized, put the gel in a plastic bag surrounded by a wet paper towel and store in 4°C.

**Running SDS Page gel:**

1. Dilute 4X SDS sample buffer (final concentration 1X), β-mercaptoethanol (final concentration 3%) with your protein sample up to a total volume of 20 µl. Mix well to combine.
2. Heat your samples at 95°C for 10 min, then quick-spin to pull down your sample.
3. Put your gel in the gel chamber, then fill the chamber with a running buffer (1X MOPS or 1X MES).
4. Connect the chamber to the voltage supplier and run your gel at 200 V for 40 min.
5. After the run ends Pry-open the gel cassette to extract your gel for future application. (You can store the used running buffer and reuse it for up to 3 times).

## Coomassie Staining

1. For Fairbanks staining buffer mix Coomassie brilliant blue R-250 (final = 0.05%), Isopropanol (final = 25%), and acetic acid (final = 10%) in a total volume of 1 or 2 L.
2. Pour the Fairbanks buffer into a staining rack, then submerge the gel in the buffer. (There should be enough buffer to cover the gel.)
3. Microwave the gel in the buffer for 1 min or until it almost boils, then let it shake on a shaker for 10 min.
4. After 10 min, store the staining solution in a new bottle.
5. Add the destaining solution (10% acetic acid in 1 or 2 L of water) to remove excess stain (Add some Kimwipes below the gel to help absorb the excess stain).
6. Heat again for 1 min or until almost boiling and let it shake for 20 min. (10 min is enough, but 20 min gives less background)
7. Image your gel using an imager or a simple background light.

## Western Blot

### Sample preparation (Western blot)

1. Prepare a lysis buffer using 1% Triton-X, sodium fluoride (1 M), sodium orthovanadate (100 mM), sodium pyrophosphate (200 mM), 1X Protease inhibitor, 150 mM NaCl, and 50 mM Tris pH 8.0, all diluted in water. (Make a large batch, then freeze in a -20°C freezer in aliquots)
2. Spin down suspension cell culture and discard the supernatant.
3. Estimate the volume of the cell pellet and add an ice-cold lysis buffer equal to 10 times the volume of the pellet of the bacterial and yeast culture. (For example, after discarding the media, if you have about 10 ul of pellets, add 100 µl of buffer).
4. Vortex for 10 sec and incubate on ice for 15 min.
5. It may be necessary to use sonication to completely lyse the yeast culture.
6. For sonication, use a program set up of 35% amplitude, 5 sec pulse on, 10 sec pulse off, for 1-3 min, and make sure to keep the sample on ice.
7. Spin down the lysate at 17,000 xg for 10 min at 4°C, then collect the supernatant for downstream application.

### Running SDS Page gel

1. Measure the concentration of your protein sample using a nanodrop
2. Dilute 4X SDS sample buffer (final concentration 1X), β-mercaptoethanol (final concentration 3%) with your protein sample up to a total volume of 20 µl. Mix well to combine. (Make sure that at the end of your dilution, you have an equal protein concentration in all of your samples.)
3. Heat your samples at 95°C for 10 min, then quick spin to pull down your sample.
4. Put your gel in the gel chamber, then fill the chamber with a running buffer (1X MOPS or 1X MES).
5. run the electrical current on 80V until the sample enters the gel, then increase the voltage to 180V for 1-2 hours.
6. After the run end Pry open the gel cassette to extract for gel transfer

### Nitrocellulose Membrane Transfer

1. Prepare a nitrocellulose membrane of the same size as your gel, two filter papers, and 1X of your transfer buffer.
2. Assemble your gel, membrane, and filter papers in the form of a sandwich in the following order: (Wet your membrane and your filter papers before adding them to the sandwich)
   1. filter paper
   2. Membrane
   3. Gel
   4. filter paper

   (Make sure that your gel is closer to the negative electrode (black side of the transfer case) and the membrane closer to the positive electrode, because the protein will migrate from negative to positive)
3. When adding your sandwich layers, make sure to use a roller to roll over each layer gently to remove trapped air bubbles.
4. Close the transfer case, making sure not to move any of the layers, then put it in the transfer chamber.
5. Start the Transfer at 100 V for 60 minutes.

### Blocking

1. Remove the blot from the transfer apparatus and place it into the blocking buffer (10% skim milk in 1X TBS + 0.05% Tween-20 or 1X PBS + 0.05% Tween-20).
2. Incubate the blot for 30 minutes at room temperature, or overnight at - 4°C.
3. Remove the blocking solution. Rinse membranes in the wash buffer (1X TBS + 0.05% Tween-20 or 1X PBS + 0.05% Tween-20).
4. Membranes may be stored in a wash buffer at 4°C for up to 1 week.

### Antibodies incubation

1. Dilute the primary antibody to the recommended concentration in a fresh blocking buffer.
2. incubate for 1-2 hours at room temperature or overnight at - 4°C.
3. Wash the membrane with a washing buffer 3 times, incubating for 5 min during each wash.
4. Apply the secondary antibody and incubate for 30 min-1 hour or overnight at - 4°C.
5. Wash the membrane again with the same 3 times, incubating for 5 min during each wash.
6. Perform the detection of proteins using a detection system of choice (e.g., Chemiluminescence).
7. View the bands under UV light.

## Comet Assay (Single-Cell Gel Electrophoresis)

*(The source document opens this protocol with a five-stage workflow diagram: Exposure of cells to DNA damaging agents → Embedding of cells into agarose → Cell lysis → Electrophoresis → DNA staining and visualization — matching the numbered parts below.)*

### Methods

**Part I: Cell Preparation and Embedding**

1. Pre-coat slides with 1% normal melting point agarose and allow them to dry
2. Prepare a single-cell suspension from the cell sample
3. Mix cells with 0.5% - 1% Low Melting Point (LMP) agarose at 37°C
4. Immediately pipette the cell/agarose mixture onto the pre-coated slides and let it solidify at 4°C for 10-20 minutes

**Part II: Cell Lysis and Alkaline Treatment**

1. Immerse slides in a cold lysis buffer (pH 10, detergents - SDS/Triton X-100) for 1-2 h at 4°C
2. Place slides in an electrophoresis chamber containing a cold alkaline buffer (pH >13, NaOH/EDTA) for 20-60 min to unwind the DNA and express alkali-labile sites

**Part IV: Electrophoresis and Visualization**

1. Run electrophoresis (1.15 V/cm, ~300 mA) for 20-60 minutes in the alkaline buffer. **Note:** Ensure the temperature remains low (~4°C) to prevent further DNA damage
2. Neutralize slides with Tris-HCl buffer, pH 7.5
3. Stain with a fluorescent DNA dye (ex: Ethidium Bromide, SYBR Green, or DAPI)
4. Visualize using fluorescent microscopy — use software to analyze comet parameters: Head DNA, Tail DNA, Tail Length, and Tail Moment (product of tail length and intensity)

## RNA Extraction with TRIzol

**Note:** Throughout this protocol make sure to always wipe everything using RNAzap to avoid RNA degradation

### Cell lysis

1. Spin down suspension cell culture and discard the supernatant.
2. Add 1 ml of Trizol reagent per 5-10 x10⁶ of cells of animal or yeast culture or 1x10⁷ of cells of bacterial culture.
3. pipet up and down to homogenize, then incubate for 5 min at room temperature.

### RNA precipitation

1. add 0.2 ml of chloroform per 1 ml of TRIzol, vortex for 20 sec, then incubate for 2-3 min
2. centrifuge for 15 min @ 12x000 xg at 4°C.
3. Transfer the top aqueous layer to a new 1.5ml tube. (Make sure to avoid transferring any of the middle white interface or the bottom pink organic phase).
4. Add 0.5 ml of isopropanol per 1 ml of Trizol and incubate at room temperature for 10 min.
5. Centrifuge for 15 min @ 12x000 xg at 4°C.
6. You should see a white pellet. Discard the supernatant with a micro pipettor without touching the pellet.

### Wash

1. Resuspend the pellet in 1ml of 75% of Ethanol per 1 ml of Trizol.
2. Wash the sample by flipping the tube up and down then spin down @ 7500x g at 4°C for 5 min. (At this step, prepare an ice bucket)
3. Discard the ethanol and let the pellet dry for 5-10 min.

### Solubilize RNA

1. Resuspend in 20-50 µl of RNase-free water or 0.1 mM EDTA. Mix by pipetting up and down.

(Alternatively, to avoid potential RNA degradation during drying, after discarding the ethanol, you could spin the sample again @ 7500x g at 4°C for 5 min and discard it. Then, resuspend in 20-50 µl of RNase-free water or 0.1 mM EDTA.

2. incubate at 55-60°C for 10-15 minutes, then place the tube on ice for downstream application or store at -80 °C.
3. Determine the RNA concentration using an OD machine at absorbance 260 nm. (a ratio of A260/A280 > 1.8 is considered pure).
4. You could run your RNA sample on an agarose gel to check if the RNA sample is degraded.

### RNA Agarose gel run

1. Mix equal amounts of your RNA sample and RNA gel loading buffer.
2. Heat the sample at 70°C for 10 min, then chill immediately on ice.
3. Load your sample on 1% agarose gel, then image using UV light.

## S. cerevisiae Transformation (Electroporation Protocol)

1. Grow *S. cerevisiae* cells in YPD medium to a density of approximately 1 × 10⁷ cells/ml at 30°C.
2. Place the cultures on ice for 15 min just before harvesting. Collect the cells by centrifugation and wash the resulting pellet thrice with ice-cold sterilized water.
3. Suspend this pellet in ice-cold freezing buffer containing 0.6-2.5 M sorbitol, 5-10 mM CaCl2 and 10 mM 2-(4-[2-hydroxyethyl]-1-piperazinyl)ethanesulphonic acid (HEPES; pH 7.5) to give a density of approximately 5 × 10⁸ cells/ml.
4. Dispense aliquots (0.1 ml) of the cell suspension in 1.5-ml microcentrifuge tubes, slowly freeze them, and store by placing them directly in a −80°C freezer (cooling rate = ~10°C/min).
5. For each electroporation, quickly thaw the frozen competent cells in a water bath at 30°C (warming rate = ~200°C/min) and wash once with 1 ml of ice-cold 1.0 M sorbitol by centrifugation. Resuspend the final pellet in 1.0 M sorbitol to give a density of 1–2 × 10⁹ cells/ml.
6. Mix the cell suspension with 0.5–10.0 ng of purified plasmid DNA and then transfer to a chilled cuvette with a 0.2-cm electrode gap. Apply a high electric pulse to the cell suspension, by using the Bio-Rad Gene Pulser II with Pulse Controller Plus.
7. Immediately dilute the electroporated cells in 1 ml of ice-cold 1.0 M sorbitol and spread an aliquot (0.1–0.2 ml) on minimal selection plates. For S. cerevisiae, the minimal selection plates contain 1.0 M sorbitol as an osmotic stabilizer.
8. The transformant colonies appear within 4–6 days at 30°C.

## B. subtilis Transformation

1. Pick a single colony of B. subtilis and inoculate 5 mL LB medium. Grow overnight at 37 °C with shaking (200 rpm).
2. Dilute the overnight culture 1:100 into fresh competence medium (such as Spizizen minimal medium or defined competence medium). Grow at 37 °C, 200 rpm until OD600 ≈ 0.5.
3. Add 1–2 µg plasmid DNA or linear DNA directly to the culture. Mix gently.
4. Incubate the culture 60–90 minutes at 37 °C with shaking to allow DNA uptake and recombination.
5. After transformation, incubate the culture 30–60 minutes in a non-selective medium to allow expression of the resistance marker.
6. Plate cells onto LB agar containing the appropriate antibiotic. Incubate at 37 °C for 16-24 hours.
   - Chloramphenicol — 5 µg/mL
   - Kanamycin — 10 µg/mL
   - Spectinomycin — 100 µg/mL
   - Erythromycin — 1 µg/mL
7. Confirm successful transformation by colony PCR, sequencing, or protein expression analysis.

## B. subtilis Nanobody Expression

1. Pick a single transformant colony and inoculate (5 mL LB + antibiotic). Grow overnight at 37 °C, 200 rpm.
2. Inoculate expression culture: Dilute the overnight culture 1:100 into a fresh medium containing antibiotics (0.5 mL overnight culture + 50 mL LB + antibiotic). Grow at 37 °C with shaking until OD600 ≈ 0.6
3. Induce nanobody expression: Depending on the promoter, add inducer (Pxyl - xylose 0.5-1% or for Pgrac - IPTG 0.1-1mM). Continue incubation at 30 °C, for 4-24 hours. Lower temperatures often improve folding and secretion.
4. Harvest secreted nanobody: Centrifuge culture (5000 g, 10 min). Collect supernatant, which contains the nanobody.

## Lyophilized Plasmid Revival

1. Keep the vial sealed until ready to use
2. Centrifuge at 6,000 x g for 1 minute at 4°C
3. Open the vial and add 20 µl of sterilized water to dissolve the DNA
4. Close the lid and vortex the vial for 1 minute
5. If necessary, heat the solution at 50°C for 15 minutes to dissolve the DNA
6. Transformation of the plasmid DNA can be performed directly after completion of the steps above. Verify the plasmid sequence after each subcloning and transformation step

## S. cerevisiae Galactose Induction

1. Prepare an overnight preculture by inoculating a single colony into 2-5 mL of appropriate selective media. Incubate overnight at 30 C with shaking. If the protein to be produced is encoded in a plasmid, use knockout mix (minimal media supplemented with amino acids, except for the one you have as an auxotrophic selection marker on your plasmid). Supplement sugar to the media if it doesn't contain it already; either 2 % glucose or 2 % raffinose. Glucose media achieves higher ODs.
2. The next morning, measure OD of your preculture; dilute as necessary to get an accurate measurement and calculate back to get the original OD.
3. Refresh the cell culture; spin cells down (around 3824g, 5 minutes). Remove supernatant. Resuspend cells in selective media containing 2 % raffinose; based on the ODs calculated, add media so that an OD of 0.2 will be achieved.
4. Grow for 4-6 hours at 30 C with shaking. Optimally, an OD of 1 will be achieved.
5. To induce the cultures, add 40 % galactose solution to the culture to achieve a final galactose concentration of 2 % (or alternatively, you can vary between 0.4-4 % as needed) 6. Grow in 30 C with shaking for 20 hours, then harvest cultures.

**Media recipe:**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Raffinose | 2.0 g |
| YNB without amino acids | 0.67 g |
| Complete amino-acid mixture | according to the manufacturer's concentration/formulation |
| G418 | your validated selection concentration |
| Sterile water | to 100 mL |
