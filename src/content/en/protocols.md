## S. cerevisiae strain maintenance

- Store strains at 4 °C for 2–3 months on YPD or SC plates sealed with Parafilm.
- For long-term storage, scrape a large inoculum from a freshly grown plate and resuspend in 1 mL of sterile 15–25% glycerol. Store at –80 °C.
- To revive frozen stocks, scrape frozen stock with a sterile toothpick or bacteriological needle, and streak on the appropriate plate (YPD or SC). Avoid thawing the stock — if it has thawed, vortex well before refreezing.

## B. subtilis culturing and maintenance

- For revival from a glycerol stock, scrape a small amount and inoculate directly into 3–5 mL LB broth.
- Alternatively, pick a single colony from a plate and inoculate into 3–5 mL LB broth.
- For an overnight culture, incubate at 30–37 °C, shaking at 180–220 rpm, for 12–16 hours.

## B. subtilis sporulation

**Part 1: Media preparation**

1. Prepare DSM (Difco Sporulation Medium) — see the recipe under **Media & Stock Solution Recipes** below.

   **Note:** Always use fresh DSM, since the FeSO₄ rusts once diluted.

**Part 2: Overnight culture**

1. Inoculate a *B. subtilis* sample in 4 mL LB broth.
2. Incubate the sample overnight at 37 °C, 200 rpm.

**Part 3: Exponential growth**

1. Measure the OD₆₀₀ of the overnight culture and dilute it in LB broth to an OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.1–0.2/mL in 10 mL LB.
2. Let the cells grow to an OD₆₀₀ of 0.8/mL at 37 °C, 200 rpm.

**Part 4: Sporulation**

1. Centrifuge 10 mL of the cells at 13,000 × g for 1 minute.
2. Wash the pellet with 1x PBS.
3. Resuspend the pellet in 5 mL DSM nutrient medium at 37 °C, 200 rpm for 48–72 hours to achieve >95% sporulation.

   **Note:** Alternatively, add 25 mL of overnight bacterial culture into a 2 L flask containing 250 mL of DSM and incubate at 37 °C, 200 rpm for 48–72 hours to achieve >95% sporulation.

4. Centrifuge the culture at 10,000 × g for 10 min.
5. Wash the pellet 3 times with cold, sterile Milli-Q H₂O to remove vegetative cell debris.
6. Heat shock the suspension (80 °C for 10–20 min) to kill any remaining vegetative cells.
7. Store purified spores in sterile ddH₂O at 4 °C.

**Part 5: Validation**

1. Determine the sporulation rate (percentage of spores among total cells) by phase-contrast microscopy (100×, oil immersion).

## Agarose gel electrophoresis

**Preparing the gel:**

1. Weigh 0.8–1 g of agarose in a microwaveable beaker.
2. Add 100 mL of 1x TAE (Tris-acetate-EDTA buffer) to the agarose powder and mix.
3. Heat the mixture in the microwave until the powder is completely dissolved. Avoid overheating.
4. Cool the agarose mixture for 5 min until it reaches 50 °C.
5. Add 2 µL of red safe per 20 mL of agarose solution, then pour the solution into the gel tray and place the well comb.
6. Let it sit at room temperature for 20–30 min until it solidifies.

**Running the gel:**

1. Once solidified, put the agarose gel in the gel chamber.
2. Fill the gel chamber with 1x TAE. Make sure the gel wells are covered.
3. Slowly load 5 µL of the molecular weight ladder into the first lane of the gel.
4. Carefully add 12 µL of your samples and controls into the wells of the gel.
5. Run the gel at 100–120 V.
6. Turn off the power, disconnect the electrodes, and slowly remove the gel from the gel box.
7. Visualize the DNA fragments (bands) with any UV light machine.

## SDS-PAGE

**Preparing the gel (5 gels):**

1. For the resolving buffer, mix 3x gel buffer, 30% acrylamide (29.65 mL), 10% APS (300 µL), and TEMED (30 µL) — total volume 30 mL.
2. Set the gel cassette vertically on a rack, then add the resolving buffer up to the 3rd horizontal line. Tap the cassette against the bench to remove air bubbles.
3. Carefully add isopropanol or 70% ethanol on top and let it polymerize for 45 min – 1 h.
4. Once the resolving gel has polymerized, carefully discard the isopropanol/ethanol layer.
5. For the stacking buffer, mix 3x gel buffer, 30% acrylamide (7.9 mL), 10% APS (80 µL), and TEMED (16 µL) — total volume 8 mL.
6. Add 1.5 mL of the stacking buffer on top of the resolving buffer.
7. Insert the comb into the gel cassette and let the gel polymerize for 30 min – 1 h.
8. Once polymerized, put the gel in a plastic bag surrounded by a wet paper towel and store at 4 °C.

**Running the gel:**

1. Dilute 4X SDS sample buffer (final 1X) and β-mercaptoethanol (final 3%) with your protein sample to a total volume of 20 µL. Mix well.
2. Heat the samples at 95 °C for 10 min, then quick-spin to pull down the sample.
3. Load the gel into the gel chamber, then fill the chamber with running buffer (1X MOPS or 1X MES).
4. Connect the chamber to the power supply and run the gel at 200 V for 40 min.
5. After the run ends, open the gel cassette to extract the gel for downstream application.

## Coomassie staining

1. For Fairbanks staining buffer, mix Coomassie brilliant blue R-250 (final 0.05%), isopropanol (final 25%), and acetic acid (final 10%) in a total volume of 1–2 L.
2. Pour the Fairbanks buffer into a staining rack and submerge the gel — there should be enough buffer to cover it.
3. Microwave the gel in the buffer for 1–5 min or until it almost boils, then shake on a shaker for 10 min.
4. After 10 min, store the staining solution in a new bottle.
5. Add the destaining solution (10% acetic acid or water) to remove excess stain (Kimwipes placed below the gel help absorb it).
6. Heat again for 5–10 min or until almost boiling and shake for 20 min.
7. Image the gel using an imager or a simple background light.

## Western blot

### Sample preparation

1. Prepare a lysis buffer: 1% Triton-X, 1 M sodium fluoride, 100 mM sodium orthovanadate, 200 mM sodium pyrophosphate, 1X protease inhibitor, 150 mM NaCl, and 50 mM Tris pH 8.0, all diluted in water. (Make a large batch and freeze in –20 °C aliquots.)
2. Spin down the suspension cell culture and discard the supernatant.
3. Estimate the pellet volume and add ice-cold lysis buffer equal to 10x the pellet volume, for both bacterial and yeast cultures (e.g. ~10 µL pellet → 100 µL buffer).
4. Vortex for 10 sec and incubate on ice for 15 min.
5. Sonication may be necessary to completely lyse yeast cultures.
6. For sonication: 35% amplitude, 5 sec pulse on / 10 sec pulse off, for 1–3 min, keeping the sample on ice throughout.
7. Spin down the lysate at 17,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C, then collect the supernatant for downstream application.

### Running the SDS-PAGE gel

1. Measure the protein concentration using a nanodrop.
2. Dilute 4X SDS sample buffer (final 1X) and β-mercaptoethanol (final 3%) with your protein sample to a total volume of 20 µL, making sure all samples end up at an equal protein concentration.
3. Heat the samples at 95 °C for 10 min, then quick-spin.
4. Load the gel into the gel chamber, then fill with running buffer (1X MOPS or 1X MES).
5. Run at 80 V until the sample enters the gel, then increase to 180 V for 1–2 hours.
6. After the run ends, open the gel cassette to extract the gel for transfer.

### Nitrocellulose membrane transfer

1. Prepare a nitrocellulose membrane the same size as the gel, two filter papers, and 1X transfer buffer.
2. Assemble the sandwich in order — filter paper, membrane, gel, filter paper — wetting the membrane and filter papers first. Keep the gel closer to the negative electrode (black side) and the membrane closer to the positive electrode, since protein migrates negative → positive.
3. Roll gently over each layer while assembling to remove trapped air bubbles.
4. Close the transfer case without shifting the layers, then place it in the transfer chamber.
5. Run the transfer at 100 V for 60 minutes.

### Blocking

1. Remove the blot from the transfer apparatus and place it in blocking buffer (10% skim milk in 1X TBS + 0.05% Tween-20, or 1X PBS + 0.05% Tween-20).
2. Incubate for 30 minutes at room temperature, or overnight at 4 °C.
3. Remove the blocking solution and rinse the membrane in wash buffer (1X TBS + 0.05% Tween-20, or 1X PBS + 0.05% Tween-20).
4. Membranes may be stored in wash buffer at 4 °C for up to 1 week.

### Antibody incubation

1. Dilute the primary antibody to the recommended concentration in fresh blocking buffer.
2. Incubate for 1–2 hours at room temperature, or overnight at 4 °C.
3. Wash the membrane with wash buffer 3 times, 5 min per wash.
4. Apply the secondary antibody and incubate for 30 min – 1 hour, or overnight at 4 °C.
5. Wash again, 3 times, 5 min per wash.
6. Detect proteins using a detection system of choice (e.g. chemiluminescence).
7. View the bands under UV light.

## Lyophilized plasmid revival

1. Keep the vial sealed until ready to use.
2. Centrifuge at 6,000 × g for 1 minute at 4 °C.
3. Open the vial and add 20 µL of sterilized water to dissolve the DNA.
4. Close the lid and vortex the vial for 1 minute.
5. If necessary, heat the solution at 50 °C for 15 minutes to dissolve the DNA.
6. Transform the plasmid DNA directly after completing the steps above. Verify the plasmid sequence after each subcloning and transformation step.

## S. cerevisiae transformation / recombineering (LiAc/PEG method)

### Part 1: Competent cell preparation

1. Inoculate 10 mL of YPD with a single colony from an *S. cerevisiae* starter plate.
2. Grow overnight at 28–30 °C in a shaking incubator (200–300 rpm).
3. Determine the OD₆₀₀.
4. Dilute cells from the overnight culture to an OD₆₀₀ of 0.3 in a total volume of 10 mL of YPD.
5. Grow the cells for 1.5–2 h at 28–30 °C in a shaking incubator until the OD₆₀₀ reaches 0.7.
6. Pellet the cells by centrifugation at 1500 rpm for 10 minutes at RT.
7. Discard the supernatant.
8. Resuspend in sterile MilliQ dH₂O.
9. Pellet the cells again by centrifugation at 1500 rpm for 10 minutes at RT.
10. Discard the supernatant.
11. Resuspend the cell pellet in 3 mL of sterile 1X TE/LiAc solution (10 mM Tris-HCl, 1 mM EDTA + 100 mM LiAc).
12. Divide the sample into 15 cryovials, 200 µL of competent cells each.
13. Place the cryovials into a styrofoam rack and freeze slowly in a –80 °C freezer.

### Part 2: Restriction digestion

**Note:** Required only for plasmids that will be integrated into the genome — skip this part for episomal plasmids.

1. Set up the restriction digestion at 37 °C for 1–16 h, depending on the enzyme.
2. In the meantime, prepare a 0.8% agarose gel (50 mL, 0.4 g agarose, 2.5 µL of 10 mg/mL EtBr).
3. Heat-inactivate the reaction by incubating for 20 min at 65 °C, if applicable.
4. Run the gel at 120 V for 50 min.

### Part 3: Selection plate preparation

1. Prepare YPD agar plates with G418, or Sc–Ura plates, depending on the plasmid's selection marker.
2. Thaw G418 on ice.

### Part 4: Heat-shock transformation

1. For each transformation, thaw one tube of competent cells at room temperature and aliquot 50 µL into a sterile microcentrifuge tube, or use 50 µL of fresh competent cells.
2. Add 0.1–5 µg of vector DNA to the competent cells, depending on the construct.

   **Note:** Linearized constructs need a higher DNA concentration (1–5 µg) for efficient transformation.

3. Add ~360 µL of transformation mix, consisting of:
   - 240 µL PEG 3350 [50% (w/v)]
   - 36 µL LiAc (1.0 M, 10x stock)
   - 50 µL salmon sperm single-stranded DNA (10.0 mg/mL), boiled for 10 min at 95 °C
   - 46.6 µL plasmid DNA

   **Note:** Mix the DNA and salmon sperm ssDNA first, then add the competent cells, then the rest of the components.

4. Incubate the transformation reactions for 30 min at 30 °C, 200 rpm.
5. Add molecular biology-grade DMSO to a final concentration of 10% (42 µL) and mix by gentle inversion.
6. Heat shock for 15–30 min at 42 °C (30 min was used this run).
7. Chill on ice for 2 min.
8. Centrifuge for 15 sec at 14,000 rpm.
9. Remove the supernatant.
10. Add 1 mL of YPD (or YPAD if using the L40 yeast strain) to each tube.
11. Incubate in a 30 °C shaker for 1 hour to allow expression of antibiotic resistance.
12. After the 1-hour recovery, pellet the cells by centrifugation at 3000 × g for 5 minutes at room temperature.
13. Discard the supernatant.
14. Resuspend the cell pellet in 1 mL of YPD broth.
15. Using a sterile spreader, plate the entire transformation on plates containing the appropriate selection marker.
16. Incubate the plates for 2–4 days at 30 °C. Each transformation should yield roughly 100 colonies, depending on the strain.

## P. pastoris recombineering (electroporation)

### Part 1: Revival

1. From a glycerol stock, inoculate 20–40 µL of PichiaPink in 10 mL of YPD media.
2. Incubate at 30 °C for 48 h, shaking at 250 rpm.
3. Spread the culture on a YPD agar plate.
4. Incubate at 30 °C for 48–72 h.

### Part 2: Starter culture

1. Inoculate 1 colony into a 125 mL sterile flask (10 mL of YPD media).
2. Incubate at 30 °C for 16–48 h, shaking at 250 rpm.

### Part 3: Restriction digestion

1. Set up the restriction digestion at 37 °C for 1–16 h, depending on the enzyme.
2. In the meantime, prepare a 0.8% agarose gel (50 mL, 0.4 g agarose, 2.5 µL of 10 mg/mL EtBr).
3. Heat-inactivate the reaction by incubating for 20 min at 65 °C, if applicable.
4. Run the gel at 120 V for 50 min.

### Part 4: DNA clean-up

1. Clean up the DNA from salts using whichever method you prefer — this protocol used the QIAquick PCR Purification Kit.

### Part 5: Competent cell preparation

1. Inoculate 1 mL of starter culture into a 1 L flask with 100 mL YPD media. Grow for 1–2 days.
2. The next day, transfer the cells into two 50 mL sterile centrifuge tubes. Centrifuge at 4 °C, 1500 g, for 5 minutes. Resuspend each pellet with ice-cold water (fill the tube and mix gently to wash the pellet), then centrifuge and remove the water — wash a second time the same way.
3. Resuspend the pellets in 10 mL of sterile ice-cold sorbitol (1 M). Centrifuge and remove the supernatant.
4. Resuspend the pellets in 300 µL of sorbitol for a final volume of 500 µL.
5. Keep the cells on ice and use them the same day for the transformation.

### Part 6: Transformation by electroporation

1. Mix 80 µL of freshly prepared cells with 5 µg of linearized plasmid and transfer to an ice-cold electroporation cuvette.
2. Incubate 5 min on ice.
3. Pulse the cells in an electroporator (this protocol used the *E. coli* option 3 on a BioRad electroporator).
4. Immediately after pulsing, add 1 mL of ice-cold YPDS media to the cuvette and mix by pipetting up and down.
5. Incubate the cells for at least 2–4 h at 30 °C without shaking, directly in the cuvette.
6. Spread the cells on selection plates and incubate at 30 °C for 3 days.

## Electroporation cuvette cleaning procedure

1. Immediately after electroporation:
   - Aspirate the cell suspension.
   - Rinse the cuvette 5–10 times with large volumes (1–2 mL each) of deionized water.
   - Flush water between the electrodes thoroughly.
2. Ethanol wash:
   - Fill the cuvette with 70% ethanol.
   - Let sit for 5–10 minutes.
   - Discard and rinse once or twice with sterile water.
3. Final rinse:
   - Rinse 3–5 times with nuclease-free water to remove any ethanol residue.
4. Dry completely:
   - Shake out excess liquid.
   - Air-dry with the opening facing downward in a dust-free environment.
   - Alternatively, blow filtered compressed air through the gap.
   - Ensure no visible droplets remain between the electrodes before storage or reuse.
5. Storage:
   - Store dry in a clean, sealed container or sterile bag.
   - Avoid touching the electrode surfaces.

**Note:** Community reports describe successful reuse for approximately 5–10 transformations, sometimes more, although transformation efficiency can gradually decline and the chance of arcing increases.

Source: [How many times can I use an electroporation cuvette? — ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/post/How_many_times_can_I_use_an_electroporation_cuvette_image_inside)

## B. subtilis recombineering

### Media components

- SM1 medium (10× stock, diluted to 1× before use)
- SM2 medium (1× stock)
- Glucose stock solution (200 g/L)
- Calcium chloride (CaCl₂) stock solution (1 M)
- Luria-Bertani (LB) medium
- LB agar plates supplemented with appropriate antibiotics

### Part 1: Preparation of overnight culture

1. Prepare SM1 medium supplemented with glucose to a final concentration of 5 g/L.
2. Inoculate 10 mL of SM1 medium with a single colony of *B. subtilis*.
3. Incubate overnight at 37 °C with shaking at 250 rpm.
4. Do not exceed 16 hours of incubation, to maintain transformation efficiency.

### Part 2: Preparation of competent cells

1. Measure the OD₆₀₀ of the overnight culture.
2. Dilute the culture in fresh SM1 medium to a final OD₆₀₀ of 0.5 in a 250 mL flask.
3. Incubate at 37 °C with shaking at 250 rpm for 3 hours.
4. Add 10 mL of SM2 medium to the culture.
5. Supplement the culture with:
   - 250 µL of 200 g/L glucose stock
   - 45 µL of 1 M CaCl₂ (added while gently swirling)
6. Continue incubation at 37 °C with shaking at 250 rpm for 2 hours.

   **Note:** Cells are considered competent for approximately 1 hour following this step.

### Part 3: Transformation

1. Aliquot 500 µL of competent cells into sterile microcentrifuge tubes.
2. Add 250 ng – 1 µg of plasmid DNA to each aliquot.
3. Incubate the mixtures at 37 °C for 30 minutes with shaking (~800 rpm).

### Part 4: Recovery

1. Add 300 µL of LB medium to each transformation reaction.
2. Incubate at 37 °C with shaking (~800 rpm) for at least 2 hours to allow expression of antibiotic resistance.

### Part 5: Plating and selection

1. Plate up to 200 µL of each transformation mixture onto LB agar plates containing the appropriate antibiotic.
2. Incubate plates overnight at 37 °C.

**Note:** For increased transformation efficiency, add an equal volume of 50% glycerol to competent cells and store at –80 °C for future use.

## UV-B / UV-C irradiation

### UV-B exposure

Equipment: UV-B transilluminator/crosslinker.

Methods (*S. cerevisiae*):

1. Grow yeast overnight in YPD at 30 °C.
2. Dilute to mid-log phase: OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.6–0.8.
3. Harvest cells by centrifugation.
4. Rinse with PBS twice.
5. Resuspend in PBS at ~1 × 10⁸ cells/mL, as a thin layer to prevent drying. Add PBS only if irradiation times are longer than 1 min, to prevent the cells from drying out.
6. Remove the plate lid to avoid plastic blocking UV-B/C.
7. Irradiate the samples with a defined dose (several groups at different dosage exposure levels).
8. Add fresh media.
9. Incubate for recovery → CFU count / viability assay.

**Note:** For bacterial cells, the protocol is identical; only the media changes to LB.
**Note:** UV-B radiation settings — 302 nm, 311 nm.

Source: [PMC10324949](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10324949/), [ScienceDirect S2666166724000030](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666166724000030)

### UV-C exposure

Equipment: UV crosslinker (available at KAUST, e.g. Christian R. Voolstra), Stratalinker 2400, UVP CL-1000, UV lamp (not ideal), UV-C LEDs.

Same method as UV-B exposure above. UV-C radiation setting: 254 nm.

## X-ray irradiation

Equipment: CIx1 Strahl operation machine.

Radiation dose range: 0–327.67 Gy. Sample preparation is the same as for UV-B exposure above.

1. Turn the key to "Power".
2. Enter credentials (restart if this takes a long time).
3. Long warm-up (600 s), then turn the key (ray icon) + green button (X-ray on).
4. On the X-ray panel, change time to 60 s.
5. Move the tray all the way up.
6. Set dose and SSD using the calibration table below:

| SSD (mm) | Dose rate (Gy/min) |
|---|---|
| 13 | 5.59 |
| 18 | 3.01 |
| 23 | 1.89 |
| 28 | 1.29 |
| 33 | 0.95 |
| 38 | 0.71 |

7. Turn the key to the ray icon and press the green button.

   **Important:** You need to see the last value provided by the machine.

8. Go to Dosimeter. In "Measured dose," write the dose rate (e.g. 5.59); in "Integral Dose," enter the last value the machine provided (e.g. 5.04). Turn to ~, press calculate, then reset, then turn the key to the ray icon and press the green button again.
9. Repeat on the X-ray panel until the value matches the dose rate, or is up to 0.1 Gy higher (e.g. between 5.59 and 5.69).
10. Repeat for every tray position.
11. Once calibrated, put the tray in the third position (SSD = 23 mm).
12. Turn off "time" on the X-ray panel.
13. Turn on "dose" and enter the desired dose.
14. Place the cells in the middle of the tray, one at a time.
15. Close the door, turn the key to the ray icon, press the green button, and wait (higher doses take longer).
16. Repeat, turning the power to "N" every time the door is opened.

## Comet assay (single-cell gel electrophoresis)

![Comet assay workflow: exposure of cells to DNA-damaging agents → embedding of cells into agarose → cell lysis → electrophoresis → DNA staining and visualization](https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6341/wiki/content/protocols/comet-assay-workflow.avif)

### Part 1: Cell preparation and embedding

1. Pre-coat slides with 1% normal-melting-point agarose and allow them to dry.
2. Prepare a single-cell suspension from the cell sample.
3. Mix cells with 0.5–1% low-melting-point (LMP) agarose at 37 °C.
4. Immediately pipette the cell/agarose mixture onto the pre-coated slides and let it solidify at 4 °C for 10–20 minutes.

### Part 2: Cell lysis and alkaline treatment

1. Immerse slides in cold lysis buffer (pH 10, detergents — SDS/Triton X-100) for 1–2 h at 4 °C.
2. Place slides in an electrophoresis chamber containing cold alkaline buffer (pH >13, NaOH/EDTA) for 20–60 min to unwind the DNA and expose alkali-labile sites.

### Part 3: Electrophoresis and visualization

1. Run electrophoresis (1.15 V/cm, ~300 mA) for 20–60 minutes in the alkaline buffer.

   **Note:** Keep the temperature low (~4 °C) to prevent further DNA damage.

2. Neutralize slides with Tris-HCl buffer, pH 7.5.
3. Stain with a fluorescent DNA dye (e.g. ethidium bromide, SYBR Green, or DAPI).
4. Visualize using fluorescent microscopy. Use software to analyze comet parameters: Head DNA, Tail DNA, Tail Length, and Tail Moment (the product of tail length and intensity).

## B. subtilis freezing/thawing

1. Inoculate *B. subtilis* in LB broth and grow 16–18 h at 37 °C (overnight) to mid-log phase (OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.5–0.8).
2. Prepare the glycerol mix by combining the liquid culture with glycerol stock to a final glycerol concentration of 20%.

6. Retrieve the vial from –80 °C and put it into an ice bucket.

2. Prepare the mixture by combining glycerol and the sample to a 15–25% glycerol concentration.
3. Mix gently.
4. Aliquot into 1 mL cryovials.
5. Place into –80 °C (snap-freezing is acceptable).
6. Retrieve the vial from –80 °C and put it into an ice bucket.
7. Use a sterile loop/pipette tip to scrape a small amount of sample onto YPD agar.

## Colony forming unit (CFU) assay (S. cerevisiae / Bacillus)

3. Incubate (12–72 h).
4. Count colonies → calculate CFU/mL.

## FUN-1 viability assay (S. cerevisiae, P. pastoris)

Live cells convert FUN-1 into red/orange cylindrical intravacuolar structures (CIVS); dead cells remain a diffuse yellow-green.

1. Grow yeast to late log phase (usually 10⁷–10⁸ cells/mL) at 30 °C in YPD broth.
2. Add 50 µL of the yeast culture to 1 mL of sterile, 0.2 µm–filtered water containing 2% D-(+)-glucose and 10 mM Na-HEPES (pH 7.2) in a microfuge tube.
3. Concentrate by centrifugation for 5 minutes at 10,000 × g in a microcentrifuge.
4. Remove the supernatant and resuspend the pellet in 1 mL of the same sterile, filtered glucose/Na-HEPES water.

   Alternative: resuspend cells in 2% D-(+)-glucose and PBS instead.

5. Combine the yeast cell suspension (10⁶–10⁷ cells/mL) with FUN-1 cell stain and, if desired, Calcofluor White M2R staining solution. Final concentrations: FUN-1 at 5–20 µM, Calcofluor White M2R at 25 µM.
6. Mix gently.
7. Incubate at 30 °C in the dark for 30 minutes, preferably with shaking.
8. Trap 5 µL of the stained suspension between a slide and an 18 mm × 18 mm coverslip.
9. Observe using a fluorescence microscope, flow cytometer, or fluorescence plate reader.

**Notes:**
- For flow cytometry, use a 488 nm laser line with standard FITC and PE channels for two-color detection of green (dead/metabolically inactive) and red (live, metabolically active) emission.
- For a plate reader: ~485 nm excitation/~530 nm emission (green) and ~485 nm excitation/~620 nm emission (red).
- Storage: –20 °C, desiccated, protected from light.
- Before using, allow reagents to warm to room temperature and centrifuge briefly before opening the vials (~3 min at 10,000 × g).
- Before refreezing, seal all vials tightly — stock solutions are stable for at least one year when stored properly.

## Resazurin viability assay (Bacillus)

1. Culture Bacillus in liquid broth overnight at 37 °C.
2. Dilute the culture in fresh LB broth to a standardized concentration.
3. Plate setup: add 100 µL of the bacterial suspension to the wells of a 96-well plate.
4. Add the treatment → incubate the plate at 37 °C for 12–24 hours.
5. Add 20 µL of resazurin solution (0.015% w/v, or 0.15 mg/mL) to each well (10% of the initial volume).
6. Incubate for an additional 1–2 hours at 37 °C in the dark.
7. Measure fluorescence (Ex 545 nm / Em 590 nm) or absorbance (570 nm vs 600 nm).

Viable cells reduce resazurin (blue, low fluorescence) to resorufin (pink, high fluorescence) via NADH/H⁺ → NAD⁺/H₂O metabolic activity.

## RNA extraction with TRIzol

**Note:** Throughout this protocol make sure to always wipe everything using RNAzap to avoid RNA degradation.

### Cell lysis

1. Spin down suspension cell culture and discard the supernatant.
2. Add 1 mL of Trizol reagent per 5-10 x10⁶ of cells of animal or yeast culture or 1x10⁷ of cells of bacterial culture.
3. pipet up and down to homogenize, then incubate for 5 min at room temperature.

### RNA precipitation

1. add 0.2 mL of chloroform per 1 mL of TRIzol, vortex for 20 sec, then incubate for 2–3 min
2. centrifuge for 15 min @ 12,000 × g at 4 °C.
3. Transfer the top aqueous layer to a new 1.5 mL tube. (Make sure to avoid transferring any of the middle white interface or the bottom pink organic phase).
4. Add 0.5 mL of isopropanol per 1 mL of Trizol and incubate at room temperature for 10 min.
5. Centrifuge for 15 min @ 12,000 × g at 4 °C.
6. You should see a white pellet. Discard the supernatant with a micro pipettor without touching the pellet.

### Wash

1. Resuspend the pellet in 1 mL of 75% of Ethanol per 1 mL of Trizol.
2. Wash the sample by flipping the tube up and down then spin down @ 7500 × g at 4 °C for 5 min. (At this step, prepare an ice bucket)
3. Discard the ethanol and let the pellet dry for 5–10 min.

### Solubilize RNA

1. Resuspend in 20-50 µl of RNase-free water or 0.1 mM EDTA. Mix by pipetting up and down.

(Alternatively, to avoid potential RNA degradation during drying, after discarding the ethanol, you could spin the sample again @ 7500 × g at 4 °C for 5 min and discard it. Then, resuspend in 20-50 µl of RNase-free water or 0.1 mM EDTA.

2. incubate at 55–60 °C for 10–15 minutes, then place the tube on ice for downstream application or store at -80 °C.
3. Determine the RNA concentration using an OD machine at absorbance 260 nm. (a ratio of A260/A280 > 1.8 is considered pure).
4. You could run your RNA sample on an agarose gel to check if the RNA sample is degraded.

### RNA Agarose gel run

1. Mix equal amounts of your RNA sample and RNA gel loading buffer.
2. Heat the sample at 70 °C for 10 min, then chill immediately on ice.
3. Load your sample on 1% agarose gel, then image using UV light.

## P. pastoris payload expression

### Part 1: BMGY starter culture

1. Pick a single transformed colony and inoculate 25 mL BMGY medium.
2. Grow for 12–48 h at 30 °C, 250 rpm.
3. Centrifuge the culture at 1500–3000 g for 10 min.
4. Discard the supernatant.

### Part 2: BMMY production culture

1. Resuspend the pellet in 500 mL – 1 L of BMMY media, with a final methanol concentration of 0.5–1%.
2. Grow for 24 h at 30 °C, 250 rpm.
3. Take a 1 mL sample for SDS-PAGE.
4. Feed the culture with methanol to a final concentration of 0.5–1%.
5. Grow for 24 h at 30 °C, 250 rpm.
6. Repeat for 24 h more.

**Note:** Total induction time should be around 2–5 days. Feed the cells exactly 0.5–1% methanol every 12–24 h.

## Harvesting cells

### Option 1: Pellet collection (intracellular proteins)

1. Turn on the high-speed centrifuge in advance; load the rotor and cool it to 4 °C.
2. Pour the culture into a 1 L ultracentrifuge bottle.
3. Balance the centrifuge bottles against each other.
4. Centrifuge at 6000–8000 g for 20 minutes.
5. Discard the supernatant.
6. Add enough ice-cold 1X PBS to resuspend the pellet.
7. Loosen the pellet using a cell scraper.
8. Pour the mixture into labeled 50 mL falcon tubes, balancing tubes against each other.
9. Spin in a tabletop centrifuge for 15 min at 4 °C, max speed.
10. Discard the supernatant.
11. Record the tube's new weight with the pellet to calculate pellet weight.
12. Store the pellet at –80 °C.

**Note:** Keep everything on ice at every step of this protocol.

### Option 2: Media collection (extracellularly secreted proteins)

1. Turn on the high-speed centrifuge in advance; load the rotor and cool it to 4 °C.
2. Pour the culture into a 1 L ultracentrifuge bottle.
3. Balance the centrifuge bottles against each other.
4. Centrifuge at 6000–8000 g for 20 minutes.
5. Collect the supernatant.
6. Add protease inhibitors to the collected media.
7. Pass the media over miracloth.
8. Centrifuge again at 6000–8000 g for 20 minutes.
9. Collect the supernatant.
10. Filter the collected media through a 0.22–0.4 µm membrane filter.
11. Purify the same day — apply the solution directly onto the AKTA.

**Note:** Keep everything on ice at every step of this protocol.

## ÄKTA HisTrap purification

### Part 1: Pellet lysis

1. Prepare fresh lysis buffer, chilled in the cold room and on ice:
   - ~5 mL of lysis buffer per 1 g of pellet.
   - 60 mL His-binding buffer + 1 tablet of EDTA-free protease inhibitor per 100 mL + 0.1 mM DTT or TCEP + 3 µL benzonase per 100 mL.

   **Note:** Add DTT/TCEP right before using the buffer.

   If desired, do a low-speed centrifugation first to pellet cell debris (6,000 × g for 10 min; can be done in falcon tubes).

2. Turn on the cell disruptor's chiller, cool the ultracentrifuge, and thaw the pellet on ice.
3. Wash the cell disruptor once with ethanol, then three times with MilliQ water.
4. Wash the cell disruptor with binding buffer.
5. Add 30–40 mL of lysis buffer to the pellet and scrape to loosen it.
6. Vortex thoroughly until homogeneous.

   **Note:** It's extremely important that no clumps remain.

7. Pour the mixture into the cell disruptor, adjust pressure to 15 kPa, and run.

   **Note:** For *S. cerevisiae* and *P. pastoris*, use 30 kPa.

8. Pour the lysate into ultracentrifuge bottles (balanced, with lids).
9. Run the ultracentrifuge at 4 °C, 50,000 g, for 45 minutes.
10. Collect the supernatant (cell lysate).

## B. subtilis nanobody expression

1. Pick a single transformant colony and inoculate 5 mL LB + antibiotic. Grow overnight at 37 °C, 200 rpm.
2. Inoculate the expression culture: dilute the overnight culture 1:100 into fresh medium with antibiotics (0.5 mL overnight culture + 50 mL LB + antibiotic). Grow at 37 °C with shaking until OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.6.
3. Induce nanobody expression, depending on the promoter: Pxyl → xylose 0.5–1%, or Pgrac → IPTG 0.1–1 mM. Continue incubation at 30 °C for 4–24 hours. Lower temperatures often improve folding and secretion.
4. Harvest secreted nanobody: centrifuge the culture (5000 g, 10 min) and collect the supernatant, which contains the nanobody.

Source: [Nature Research Intelligence — *B. subtilis* protein expression and secretion](https://www.nature.com/research-intelligence/nri-topic-summaries/bacillus-subtilis-protein-expression-and-secretion-micro-5636), [iGEM Technion-Israel secretory-system protocol](https://static.igem.org/mediawiki/2019/e/e6/T--Technion-Israel--proto-bacisecretorysystem.pdf)

## S. cerevisiae galactose induction

1. Prepare an overnight preculture by inoculating a single colony into 2–5 mL of appropriate selective media. Incubate overnight at 30 C with shaking. If the protein to be produced is encoded in a plasmid, use knockout mix (minimal media supplemented with amino acids, except for the one you have as an auxotrophic selection marker on your plasmid). Supplement sugar to the media if it doesn't contain it already; either 2% glucose or 2% raffinose. Glucose media achieves higher ODs.
2. The next morning, measure OD of your preculture; dilute as necessary to get an accurate measurement and calculate back to get the original OD.
3. Refresh the cell culture; spin cells down (around 3824 × g, 5 minutes). Remove supernatant. Resuspend cells in selective media containing 2% raffinose; based on the ODs calculated, add media so that an OD of 0.2 will be achieved.
4. Grow for 4–6 hours at 30 C with shaking. Optimally, an OD of 1 will be achieved.
5. To induce the cultures, add 40% galactose solution to the culture to achieve a final galactose concentration of 2% (or alternatively, you can vary between 0.4–4% as needed).
6. Grow in 30 C with shaking for 20 hours, then harvest cultures.

**Media recipe:**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Raffinose | 2.0 g |
| YNB without amino acids | 0.67 g |
| Complete amino-acid mixture | according to the manufacturer's concentration/formulation |
| G418 | your validated selection concentration |
| Sterile water | to 100 mL |

## Media & stock solution recipes

Reagents and stock solutions referenced by name throughout the protocols above.

### YPD medium

**Application:** Maintain yeast strains under non-selective conditions.

For 1 L YPD:
1. 20 g Bacteriological peptone
2. 10 g Yeast extract
3. 20 g Bacteriological agar (for plates)
4. Add 960 mL of deionized water, autoclave for 15 minutes, and add 40 mL of a 50% sterile (0.2 µm filtered) solution of glucose.

### 10X Lithium Acetate (LiAc)

**Application:** *S. cerevisiae* transformation.

1 M lithium acetate — for 100 mL:
1. Dissolve 10.2 g of lithium acetate in 90 mL of deionized water.
2. Adjust pH to 7.5 with dilute HCl and bring the volume up to 100 mL.
3. Filter-sterilize and store at room temperature.

### 10X Tris-HCl/EDTA (TE) Buffer

**Application:** *S. cerevisiae* transformation.

100 mM Tris, 10 mM EDTA — for 100 mL:
1. Dissolve 1.21 g of Tris-HCl and 0.37 g of EDTA in 90 mL of deionized water.
2. Adjust the pH to 8.0 with NaOH and bring the volume up to 100 mL.
3. Filter-sterilize and store at room temperature.

### 50% (w/v) PEG 3350

**Application:** *S. cerevisiae* transformation.

1. Dissolve 50 g of PEG 3350 in ~30 mL of ddH₂O using heat and a stir bar until fully dissolved.
2. Adjust volume to 100 mL.
3. Filter-sterilize and store at room temperature.

### Single-Stranded Carrier DNA (2.0 mg/mL)

**Application:** *S. cerevisiae* transformation.

1. Dissolve 200 mg of salmon sperm DNA in 100 mL of TE buffer (10 mM Tris-HCl, 1 mM Na₂EDTA, pH 8.0) by stirring at 4 °C for 1–2 hr.
2. Store 1.0 mL samples at –20 °C.
3. Denature the carrier DNA in a boiling water bath for 10 min and chill in ice/water before use.
4. Boiled samples, stored at –20 °C, can be reused or reboiled three times without loss of activity.

### Sc–Ura Selective Agar

**Application:** Select yeast strains originally auxotrophic for uracil after transformation with a URA3-containing plasmid.

For 1 L media:
1. Add 6.7 g of YNB w/ Ammonium Sulfate (w/o amino acids).
2. Add 1.92 g of –Ura Mix.
3. Add 20 g bacto agar (2% w/v) for solid plate support.
4. Add 900 mL MilliQ water and autoclave the mixture.
5. Once the mixture cools down, add 2% (w/v) D-glucose from a filter-sterilized solution (e.g. if the stock is 20%, add 100 mL).
6. Pour the plates.

### Sorbitol Solution (1 M)

**Application:** *P. pastoris* electroporation.

1. Weigh 18.2 g of sorbitol.
2. Add 100 mL of MilliQ H₂O.
3. Autoclave-sterilize and store at room temperature. Stock solutions are stable for up to 6 months at 20 °C.

### 0.8% Agarose Gel

**Application:** Visualization of DNA.

1. In 50 mL of 1x TBE, dissolve 0.4 g of agarose.
2. Boil in a microwave to dissolve the agarose fully.
3. Once the solution cools down, add 2.5 µL of 10 mg/mL EtBr.
4. Pour into the mold and add the comb.

### Difco Sporulation Medium (DSM)

**Application:** Sporulation of *B. subtilis*.

1. 8 g Nutrient Broth
2. 1 g KCl
3. 1 mL MgSO₄ (1 M)
4. 1 mL MnCl₂ (10 mM)
5. 1000 mL Aqua bidest
6. Autoclave the mixture and add 0.5 mL CaCl₂ (1 M) with 1 mL FeSO₄ (1 M).
7. Add D-glucose once the medium has cooled down.

**Note:** Always use fresh DSM, since the FeSO₄ rusts once diluted.
