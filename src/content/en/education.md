## Overview

Synthetic biology and genetic engineering are transforming agriculture, healthcare and manufacturing. Yet in Saudi Arabia and the wider Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region, synthetic biology is still a new and unfamiliar discipline, especially in schools. Secondary school curricula focus on general biology, leaving a gap in synthetic biology, bioengineering and biotechnology, and most learning resources in the field exist only in English.

At KAUST iGEM, our vision goes beyond the bench. We believe public literacy and educational empowerment are essential to scientific innovation. As researchers at King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST), we are in a unique position to introduce these topics to our local community and to the next generation of Saudi scientists and innovators.

## Copy Paste Plants

### Overview

On Tuesday, April 7, 2026, KAUST iGEM ran Copy Paste Plants as part of the Sustainability Family Food Night at The KAUST School (TKS), organized by the Center of Excellence for Sustainable Food Security and TKS. The evening brought together students, families, community members and experts to explore plants, sustainable food, innovative technology and environmental science.

### The activity

Our booth ran **Copy Paste Plants**, a hands-on plant cloning activity that introduced children to the idea of genetic identity. Children learned to propagate a pothos plant from a cutting in three steps: get your plant, make the cut, and watch it grow. Because the new plant grows from the parent's own tissue, it is an exact genetic copy, which gave us a simple, tangible way to explain genetic identity and similarity to elementary-aged learners.

## Oboor Sister Program

### Why we started

Oboor (<span lang="ar" dir="rtl">عبور</span>) means “transition” in Arabic. It is a university adaptation program developed by Renad Almalki, a master’s researcher in counseling psychology at Umm Al-Qura University, as a sister program to the iGEM KAUST Summer Mentorship Program.

The transition from high school to university is a major step, and it brings uncertainty and anxiety. Oboor gives Saudi students enrolled in our Summer Mentorship Program the tools and space to cope with these anxieties and prepare for university life.

## Kallpa Multiplier Mentorship (Peru)

### Context

The use of genetically modified organisms (GMOs) has been a subject of controversy for several decades. Latin America is home to a significant portion of the world's genetically modified crops, with countries such as Brazil, Argentina and Paraguay standing out <a class="cite" href="#ref-kallpa-1">1</a>. However, other nations, such as Peru, have taken a stance of staunch opposition, seeking to safeguard their agrobiodiversity and organic gastronomy. A moratorium is currently in effect in Peru (under Law No. 31.111) that legally restricts the entry, cultivation and production of Living Modified Organisms (LMOs) <a class="cite" href="#ref-kallpa-2">2</a>.

Yet, in April 2026, Peru approved Ministerial Resolution No. D000068-2026-MINAM-DM. This resolution states that if a plant has been edited using CRISPR to modify or silence one of its own genes, without the introduction of exogenous DNA, the organism is not classified as an LMO <a class="cite" href="#ref-kallpa-3">3</a> <a class="cite" href="#ref-kallpa-4">4</a>. Consequently, it would be exempt from the moratorium, potentially allowing for its commercial cultivation.

<!-- kallpa:dialogue -->

### What we identified

Despite this significant change, farmers, students and the public reacted with concern, fear and confusion, often shaped by misinformation about the safety, environmental impact and consequences of GMOs and gene editing. To understand the situation, we partnered with Kallpa Greenz.

<div class="panel">
<div class="panel-title"><span>Impact partner</span><span>Kallpa Greenz · Peru</span></div>

Led by Darwin Paul Díaz Cancino, a winner of the tenth Start-Up Perú call for proposals and a gold medalist at the 2021 iGEM Design League (synthetic biology), Kallpa Greenz combines sustainability with social commitment. It promotes sustainable green technologies and responsible food production through hydroponics and automation, with a strong focus on R&D. The team comprises young volunteers and professionals, over 76 young professionals and students led by 8 managers, who implement hydroponic systems, bioclimatic greenhouses and photobioreactors to support vulnerable communities.

### Our educational strategy: a multiplier model

The primary objective was not to instill a specific stance on GMOs in the volunteers, but rather to provide them with the tools and knowledge necessary to understand what GMOs are, how they function, how they are applied, the factors that influence them, and the regulatory frameworks that govern them in Peru. To achieve this, we organized the content into four interconnected learning modules, featuring weekly activities that allowed the volunteers to put what they had learned into practice within their communities.
