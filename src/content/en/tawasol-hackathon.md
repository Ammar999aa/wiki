## Overview

**Tawasol** (تواصل — "connection" or "communication") is a synthetic biology policy hackathon organized by KAUST iGEM in partnership with the NYU Abu Dhabi iGEM team. Held August 28–30, 2026, it brings together university and advanced high school students from across the MENA region to design governance, ethical, and regulatory frameworks for two live research platforms: SHIHAB (KAUST) and DUNELOCK (NYU Abu Dhabi).

Rather than designing experiments, participating teams spend the weekend developing a policy whitepaper and a live pitch, addressing the questions that arise once a synthetic biology platform is ready to leave the lab: who regulates it, who trusts it, and who it is actually for.

## Why We Built This

Synthetic biology's hardest open questions are increasingly not technical, they are regulatory, ethical, and societal. Most student STEM programs train young scientists exclusively in bench science, leaving a real gap: students rarely get the chance to practice governing, regulating, or communicating the technology they are being trained to build.

Tawasol was built to close that gap, and to open a second one alongside it. For students who may not see themselves as future bench scientists, it offers a way into synthetic biology through policy, ethics, communication, and critical thinking, skills that connect directly to careers in governance, entrepreneurship, and industry, not just the lab.

We partnered with NYU Abu Dhabi deliberately, not by default. A second university and a second live research platform meaningfully widened our regional reach and gave participating students two real systems to engage with instead of one. That said, Tawasol was conceived, built, and is fully owned and operated by KAUST iGEM. The format works equally well as a standalone, single-institution program, and the partnership reflects a broader goal we have been pursuing all year: uniting previously isolated MENA iGEM teams into a connected regional community, rather than leaving each team to work in isolation.

## The Two Tracks

Tawasol is structured around a deliberate design choice: both tracks study completely different environments, but are built around the exact same four categories of governance challenge. Teams choose one track and work through all four sub-challenges within it.

### SHIHAB — Space Biomanufacturing Governance

SHIHAB is ready for deployment in a "Zero-Reach" zone, a permanent lunar research station, or an extremely remote, off-grid community cut off from supply lines. Teams design the operational, technical, and ethical charter governing how it gets trusted and deployed.

### DUNELOCK — Desert Stabilization Governance

DUNELOCK uses engineered soil-associated bacteria to stabilize shifting desert sand and reduce dust storms across dryland borders. Teams design the framework governing large-scale, open-environment deployment, where containment cannot rely on physical walls.

### Shared Sub-Challenge Structure

Both tracks are evaluated against the same four sub-challenges, applied to their respective platform:

1. **Safety & Biocontainment** — regulatory frameworks for verifying biological safety without a conventional laboratory setting.
2. **Public Trust & Health Impact** — communication, consent, and public-health strategies for community-facing biotechnology.
3. **Integration & Liability** — liability standards and infrastructure integration for real-world deployment.
4. **Sustainability & Long-Term Impact** — how the platform's deployment advances the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

> Same four challenges. Different world. That is the point: the structure is designed to show that governance thinking transfers across very different biotechnology applications.

## Program Design

Tawasol runs across three days, shifting format as the weekend progresses.

| Day | Date | Format | Key Milestone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Friday, Aug 28 | Virtual | Team formation; Problem Statement due, 10:00 PM |
| Day 2 | Saturday, Aug 29 | Virtual | Development + Mentorship Power Hour; Final Whitepaper due, 11:59 PM |
| Day 3 | Sunday, Aug 30 | Hybrid | Virtual judging, 9:00 AM–12:00 PM; in-person celebration, 1:00–5:00 PM |

Each team submits a **five-page policy whitepaper** and delivers a **five-minute live pitch** with judge Q&A, mirroring how real policy proposals are developed and defended. Teams register in groups of 3–5, with solo applicants matched into a compatible team ahead of the event.

### Evaluation

Submissions are judged against five weighted criteria, worth five points each: Technical Integration, Policy Feasibility, Ethical & Social Impact, Strategic Clarity, and Persuasive Pitch. No internal student members judge the competition; all submissions are evaluated by an external panel to maintain objectivity.

## Regional Reach

Registration was promoted across six major Saudi universities (KFUPM, King Abdulaziz University, University of Jeddah, Umm Al-Qura University, Taibah University, and King Faisal University) alongside KAUST's own community, and reached participants from across Saudi Arabia, the wider Gulf, and additional MENA institutions.

## Looking Forward

Tawasol is intended to be the first cycle of an ongoing program, not a one-time event. The same structure, aligned sub-challenges, an external judging framework, and a shared whitepaper standard, was designed from the outset to be reusable across future cycles and adoptable by other institutions in the region.
