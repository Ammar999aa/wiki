## Overview

Most conversations about synthetic biology happen in English, in labs and in academic journals. For much of the public in Saudi Arabia and the MENA region, the field remains unfamiliar, and sometimes misunderstood.

Our science communication work aims to bring synthetic biology into everyday conversation, in Arabic and English, through formats people already enjoy: films, podcasts and social media. We also work beyond our own team, uniting iGEM teams across the MENA region through the Tawasol Initiative so that the region's synthetic biology story is told together.

<h3 id="wall-e">WALL-E</h3>

#### Summary

On Thursday, May 7, 2026, we hosted an interactive screening of WALL-E at KAUST's Discovery Cinema for 70 attendees, 50 of whom stayed for the discussion that followed. The film's story of a polluted Earth and a single surviving plant opened a discussion on how synthetic biology could help address global environmental challenges.

#### Event structure

- **Mission-based crews:** on arrival, each attendee joined a themed crew, such as Biobuilders, Plant Rescue or Green Galaxy, and took on a science role within it, such as Robot Engineer, Space Pilot or Plant Scientist. This encouraged teamwork and mixed people who didn't know each other. Prompt: who is on your crew, and what is your role?
- **Synthetic biology discussion:** before the screening, attendees received a logbook with prompts on plant biology, space agriculture and ecological challenges. Afterwards, each crew discussed the film together, and we introduced real synthetic biology research, such as biodegradable plastics, bridging the gap between science fiction and active research. Prompt: could the real science behind the film already exist?
- **Operation Earth 2.0:** to close, each crew sketched its own plan to rebuild Earth using synthetic biology, space science and engineering. Prompt: how would your crew rebuild Earth?

Each crew had its own mission and its own question to answer:

<div class="card-grid row">

<div>

#### Biobuilders

Mission: engineer a solution. Which microbe would you design to break down Earth's plastic waste, and what would it need to survive?

</div>

<div>

#### Plant Rescue

Mission: save the last plant. A single plant survives on a polluted Earth. How would you help it grow and spread?

</div>

<div>

#### Green Galaxy

Mission: grow food in space. How would you farm on a spaceship, with little water, light or soil?

</div>

</div>

<h3 id="hail-mary">Project Hail Mary</h3>

#### Summary

On Sunday, August 30, 2026, we held a screening and open discussion of Project Hail Mary for 35 undergraduate and graduate students, sponsored by the KAUST BioMed division. The film follows a scientist using microbiology to save Earth, which let us move the conversation into space biology and extremophile microbiology, areas close to our own project, SHIHAB. The event began at 6:00 PM in Conference Room 0215, in the Auditorium Building between Buildings 4 and 5.

#### Event structure

- **Ethics discussion:** after the screening, we held an open discussion with the attendees about the film's main themes and the ethical questions that arise from them. Every theme opened with a "would you rather" question, then moved through scenes from the movie, and ended on one big closing question.

<div class="card-grid">

<div>

#### 1. The value of ignorance

Would you rather know the truth about everything, even if it makes you permanently unhappy, or live a happy life built on beliefs you know might be completely false?

</div>

<div>

#### 2. What makes a life meaningful?

Would you rather live an extremely meaningful life that ends at 30, or a completely ordinary life that lasts to 100?

</div>

<div>

#### 3. Human exceptionalism

Would you rather discover that humans are uniquely intelligent in the universe but fundamentally insignificant, or that humans are completely ordinary but deeply important to someone else?

</div>

<div>

#### 4. Moral responsibility

Would you rather be responsible for a terrible outcome because you tried to do the right thing, or responsible for nothing because you chose never to act?

</div>

<div>

#### 5. Individuality vs. collective good

Would you rather live in a society where everyone is free to pursue their own happiness, even if some people are left behind, or one where everyone is guaranteed a good life but nobody may put themselves significantly ahead of everyone else?

</div>

</div>

## Social media

We launched KAUST iGEM's LinkedIn, Instagram and TikTok accounts and developed our content strategy together with KAUST's Global Branding and Communications office. Our channels serve Saudi and regional youth, professionals, sponsors and partner teams.

As part of our public science communication efforts, KAUST iGEM used Instagram, TikTok, and LinkedIn to share our research, document our team's activities, and introduce synthetic biology to audiences beyond the laboratory. Each platform helped us reach a different audience, from students and young researchers to professionals working in science, education, and biotechnology. Through short videos, educational posts, and event announcements, we aimed to make our work more accessible and encourage people to learn about synthetic biology.

## Tawasol: uniting MENA iGEM teams

### Overview

Tawasol (تواصل) means "connection." Every year, iGEM teams across the MENA region do valuable outreach, often in Arabic and often for audiences hearing about synthetic biology for the first time, but most of it happens in isolation. Each team builds its own glossary, its own infographics and its own surveys, and much of that work is lost when the season ends.

Tawasol began with the <a href="/kaust-ksa/human-practices">Tawasol Hackathon</a>, which KAUST iGEM co-organized with NYU Abu Dhabi iGEM in August 2026. Building on that partnership, KAUST iGEM launched the Tawasol Initiative to connect MENA iGEM teams into a regional coalition with four goals:

<ol class="goals">

<li>

**Arabic synthetic biology education:** co-create accessible, standardized educational tools, glossaries and outreach materials in Arabic.

</li>
<li>

**Unified regional outreach:** co-design Arabic public engagement tools and culturally tailored science communication.

</li>
<li>

**Stronger Human Practices:** collaborate on bio-governance, policy and public perception in our region.

</li>
<li>

**A joint public showcase:** present the region's synthetic biology work together to local and global audiences.

</li>

</ol>

### The Tawasol summit

On September 27–28, 2026, we hosted the virtual Tawasol Summit with regional iGEM teams.

- **Day 1, Kickoff and Vision:** teams introduced themselves, we presented the Tawasol vision and the case for Arabic synthetic biology education, and each team gave a lightning preview of its project and local outreach goals.
- **Day 2, Co-working session:** teams compared public perceptions of synthetic biology across their countries, audited key synthetic biology terms to agree on how to translate and simplify them in Arabic, planned the public showcase and its shared assets, and co-authored a joint regional statement.

### The Tawasol public showcase

Building on the Summit, the Tawasol Public Showcase, planned for October 2026, will bring the region's iGEM teams together to present their work to local and global audiences, with teams unable to attend in person featured through video.
