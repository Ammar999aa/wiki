Scientific progress rarely happens in isolation. Throughout the 2026 iGEM season, we worked with fellow iGEM teams, educational organizations, community partners, and institutions to extend our project's reach beyond the laboratory. 

Our collaborations took many forms. Some partners shared their scientific knowledge through mentorship and educational workshops. Others helped us develop competitions, organize public events, or bring synthetic biology to students who might otherwise have little exposure to the field. Each collaboration let us contribute something of our own while learning from others' experience, expertise, and perspectives. 

One of our most important goals was to make synthetic biology more accessible across the Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region. We wanted to connect teams working in different countries, strengthen regional scientific communication, and create opportunities for students to engage with synthetic biology in Arabic. At the same time, we looked beyond our region, partnering with organizations in Peru and Kazakhstan and working with local communities across Saudi Arabia. 

These collaborations became an essential part of our work in education, public engagement, and human practices. 

**MENA iGEM Collaborations**  
The Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region is home to a growing community of young scientists and innovators. Yet, opportunities for iGEM teams across the region to connect, share resources, and communicate their research to broader audiences remain limited. 

We saw an opportunity to change that. 

What began as individual collaborations with other iGEM teams gradually developed into Tawasol (تواصل), a regional initiative designed to bring teams together around science communication, education, and public engagement. The name *Tawasol* means connection or communication in Arabic. It reflects our belief that scientific collaboration should extend beyond exchanging technical information. It should also create relationships, build shared resources, and make scientific knowledge accessible to the communities we serve. 

Through our partnerships with NYU Abu Dhabi, Al-Nahrain University, King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM), and Armed Forces College of Medicine (AFCM) Egypt, we worked towards a more connected regional iGEM community. 

1. **NYU Abu Dhabi iGEM: The Tawasol Hackathon and Policy Showcase**  
   Our collaboration with the NYU-AD iGEM team centered on co-organizing the Tawasol Hackathon and Policy Showcase, held August 28-30. Together, we developed a multidisciplinary competition featuring challenges inspired by our respective SHIHAB and DUNELOCK projects, encouraging participants to consider scientific innovation alongside policy, ethics, and real-world feasibility.  
     
   NYU-AD also joined the subsequent Tawasol Regional Summit and Public Showcase, helping expand our collaboration into a broader regional network.   
     
2. **Al-Nahrain University iGEM**   
   Al-Nahrain University iGEM contributed to our iGEM Mentorship Program by delivering an Artificial Intelligence in Biotechnology webinar on July 30\. Team members Ayham, Tiba, Teeba, and Tasmeen introduced participants to the intersection of AI and biological research, expanding the program’s interdisciplinary content.   
     
   Our collaboration continued through the Tawasol Regional Summit and Public Showcase, where Al-Nahrain joined other regional teams in sharing research and promoting Arabic-language science communication.   
3. **KFUPM iGEM**  
   We collaborated with KFUPM iGEM to develop and deliver ***Can We Engineer Life?***, a joint synthetic biology session for Space Academy on August 21\. Members of both teams shared teaching responsibilities, combining their expertise to introduce students to biological engineering and its potential applications.   
     
   KFUPM also participated in the Tawasol Regional Summit and Public Showcase, strengthening our connections within Saudi Arabia’s synthetic biology community. 

4. **AFCM Egypt iGEM**  
   AFCM Egypt joined our expanding Tawasol initiative, participating in the Regional Summit and Public Showcase alongside KAUST, NYU-AD, Al-Nahrain, and KFUPM.   
     
5. **NIS-Medeu**  
   We collaborated with NIS-Medeu through their BioAccelerator Program and synthetic biology seminars. Our wet lab team mentored high school students, providing feedback on project ideas, technical feasibility, and experimental design. We also served as judges for the BioAccelerator competition, evaluating and ranking student projects, and delivered several guest lectures during their synthetic biology seminars. Throughout the collaboration, we continued supporting NIS-Medeu with scientific and technical feedback on their iGEM project.

**Global Educational Collaborations**

1. **Kallpa Greenz, Peru**  
   We formed a partnership with Kallpa Greenz, a Peruvian company that builds greenhouse domes using modern technology across various climates in Peru. KALLPA has over 70 volunteers working in different areas (construction, marketing, business opportunities, logistics, education, among others). SHIHAB designed and developed a Spanish language training program on GMOs for KALLPA’s young volunteers, with the goal of transferring this knowledge to farmers in rural areas of the country, ultimately reaching approximately 180 farmers trained across three different communities.  
     
2. **Jeddah Global Shapers**  
   We connected with Jeddah Global Shapers to explore opportunities for community-based science education and youth engagement. Our discussions focused on combining our team’s scientific expertise with the organization’s community outreach network to make biotechnology more accessible to young people in Jeddah.  
     
3. **Oboor Sister Program**  
   We collaborated with Renad Almalki, a counseling psychology master’s researcher at Umm Al-Qura University, to recruit students for the Oboor Mentorship Sister Program as a companion to our iGEM Mentorship Program.   
     
   While our mentorship initiative introduced students to scientific research and academic opportunities, Oboor focused on preparing Saudi students for the personal and educational transition from high school to university. Together, the programs supported students’ scientific interests and broader academic development.   
   

**Local Community and Institutional Partnerships** 

1. **Jeddah Department of Education**  
   The Jeddah Department of Education supported our school outreach efforts by helping us connect with local educational institutions and student audiences. This partnership facilitated opportunities to introduce synthetic biology through accessible, age-appropriate educational activities, including Arabic-language workshops.  
     
2. **Zahrat Al-Sahra International School**  
   Zahrat Al-Sahra International School hosted our Biomission in Space workshop for 58 middle school students aged 12-14. Working with the school, we introduced students to synthetic biology through an interactive activity in which they designed hypothetical microorganisms for space missions, encouraging creativity and scientific problem-solving.  
     
3. **KAUST Campus & Community: Thuwal Science Pioneers Program**  
   We partnered with KAUST Campus & Community to organize the Thuwal Science Pioneers Program, bringing local middle- and high-school students onto the KAUST campus.   
     
   Campus & Community coordinated school visits and logistics, while our team developed and delivered Arabic-language synthetic biology lectures, hands-on activities, ethical discussions, and research facility tours. Together, we helped connect students from neighboring communities with KAUST’s scientific resources.  
     
4. **KAUST Center of Excellence for Sustainable Food Security & The KAUST School & The KAUST School (TKS)**  
   We collaborated with The KAUST School (TKS) and the KAUST Center of Excellence for Sustainable Food Security to introduce younger students and families to biotechnology and sustainability. Through our Copy Paste Plants workshop, we connected synthetic biology concepts with real-world applications in agriculture, food production, and environmental sustainability, encouraging hands-on learning and scientific curiosity within the KAUST community.  
     
5. **KAUST Academy**  
   Our engagement with KAUST Academy focused on connecting our educational initiatives with broader student innovation and development opportunities, particularly through discussions surrounding the Tawasol Hackathon.  
     
6. **KAUST Policy Institute**  
   The KAUST Policy Institute contributed policy expertise to the Tawasol Hackathon and Policy Showcase, supporting the evaluation of student proposals through a policy and governance perspective. Its involvement helped participants consider the regulatory, ethical, and societal dimensions of biotechnology alongside scientific feasibility.

7. **KAUST Consulting Club**  
   The KAUST Consulting Club supported the Tawasol Hackathon by contributing judges to evaluate participants’ proposals and presentations. Their involvement brought additional perspectives on problem-solving, communication, and the practical application of innovative ideas.  
     
8. **KAUST LEADs**  
   KAUST LEADs contributed judges to the Tawasol Hackathon, supporting the evaluation of student proposals and expanding the range of perspectives represented in the judging process.  
     
9. **KAUST BESE-BioMed Senate**  
   We collaborated with the KAUST BESE-BioMed Students Senate during our iGEM Mentorship Program, hosting an interactive professional development panel titled *Demystifying the SynBio Path* on August 10\. Senate members and graduate student panelists shared insights into pursuing biology-related degrees, graduate school applications, research opportunities, and student life at KAUST, helping mentees navigate their academic and scientific career paths.  
     
10. **KAUST Student Life: Orientation Expo 2026**  
    Through the 2026 KAUST Orientation Expo, organized by KAUST Student Life, we connected with incoming students and members of the university community. The event provided an opportunity to introduce iGEM, share our project, and encourage interest in synthetic biology and student-led research.  
    

**Growing Through Collaboration**   
Our collaborations showed us that scientific impact extends beyond the laboratory. Fellow iGEM teams brought new ideas and expertise, educational partners helped us reach diverse audiences, and institutional collaborators contributed the resources and perspectives needed to turn our initiatives into reality. 

From developing educational resources with Kallpa Greenz to building a five-team regional network through Tawasol, reach partnership strengthened our ability to communicate science, engage communities, and learn from others.

Together, these experiences reinforced a central lesson of our iGEM journey: meaningful scientific progress is built not only through what we discover, but through what we share.
